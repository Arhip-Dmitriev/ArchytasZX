ARCHYTASZX DOCUMENTATION
========================

Layer A: Symbolic Algebra

Applies to: ArchytasZX 2.0.x
Package: archytaszx.algebra (modules dimension, phase, scalar, algebraic)


1. PURPOSE
----------

Layer A supplies the three kinds of exact value every other layer is built from:

  - Dim, a dimension: a positive integer that may be symbolic;
  - Phase and PhaseVector, the phase data of a spider;
  - Scalar, an exact complex number that may be symbolic.

Each is immutable and hashable and is backed by one canonical sympy expression. Equality of
these values is sound and incomplete: values that compare equal are mathematically equal,
but mathematically equal values may compare unequal when the cheap canonicalisation cannot
show it. No value of this layer is ever approximated, and none of its operations discards a
factor.


2. DIMENSIONS: archytaszx.algebra.dimension
-------------------------------------------

2.1 The grammar of dimensions

A Dim is one of:

  - a concrete positive integer (>= 1);
  - a symbol, which carries the assumptions positive and integer;
  - a product of dimensions;
  - a power of a dimension by a non-negative integer exponent, the exponent itself concrete
    or a symbol (assumptions integer and non-negative).

Sums, quotients, roots and floats are outside the grammar and are rejected with
DimensionGrammarError. Values outside the domain (zero, negative integers, negative exponents)
raise DimensionDomainError.

A dimension is stored on each port of a diagram. There is no global or default dimension.

2.2 class Dim

    Dim(value: int | str | Dim)
    Dim.concrete(value: int) -> Dim
    Dim.symbol(name: str) -> Dim
    Dim.from_sympy(expr) -> Dim                 rejects anything outside the grammar

    a * b, a ** e                               products and powers
    a == b                                      d1*d2 == d2*d1, d*d == d**2

    dim.to_sympy()                              the canonical sympy expression
    dim.is_concrete -> bool                     no free symbols
    dim.free_symbols -> frozenset[str]
    dim.to_int() -> int                         the value of a concrete dimension
    dim.substitute(mapping) -> Dim              symbols replaced by integers
    dim.rewrite(mapping) -> Dim                 symbols replaced by other Dims
    dim.abstract(*, avoid=(), stem="d")         a fresh symbol for a concrete dimension and
        -> (Dim, Mapping[str, int])             the binding that recovers its value
    dim.unify(other: Dim) -> UnifyResult        decide dim == other

abstract is the inverse of substitute. It is how a user's literal 3 is made to behave as a
symbol: the fresh name avoids every name in avoid and the binding records the value.

2.3 Unification

unify decides one equation over the positive integers. It first cancels common factors, then
either decides the reduced equation or reports it as a residual constraint. Its result is a
UnifyResult with a status:

  Status    Meaning
  --------  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SUCCESS   The equation holds, possibly under the bindings reported in result.bindings.
  FAILURE   The equation has no solution over the positive integers.
  DEFERRED  The equation could not be decided; result.constraints holds the reduced pairs.

unify never reports SUCCESS for an equation it did not verify and never FAILURE for one that
has a solution. An equation with several solution branches is DEFERRED, for example a product
of symbols against a constant (d1*d2 == 6) or equal bases with symbolic exponents
(d^n == d^m).

2.4 Systems of equations

    solve(constraints: Sequence[tuple[Dim, Dim]], *, max_passes=None) -> SolveResult
    unify_all(dims: Sequence[Dim]) -> UnifyAllResult

solve resolves a system of asserted equalities to a monotone fixpoint: each pass resolves both
sides of every pair through the bindings found so far and unifies them. The result depends
only on the set of constraints. A symbol bound to two values that do not unify is FAILURE. A
converged system with residual pairs is DEFERRED with exhausted=False; a system that did not
converge within the pass budget (32 passes by default) is DEFERRED with exhausted=True.

unify_all resolves a multiset of dimensions that must all be equal. Concrete bindings are
reported in bindings, non-concrete ones in declined_bindings. Callers must distinguish an
exhausted result from an ordinary deferred one; the diagram validator treats exhaustion as a
hard error.


3. PHASES: archytaszx.algebra.phase
-----------------------------------

3.1 The mathematical model

A qudit Z spider with phase vector (alpha_1, ..., alpha_{d-1}) denotes

    sum_{k=0}^{d-1} e^{i alpha_k} |k>^(tensor n) <k|^(tensor m)

with alpha_0 = 0 fixed as gauge. Fusing two spiders of the same colour adds their phase
vectors entrywise, which is why Phase and PhaseVector support addition.

3.2 class Phase

A Phase is an angle stored in turns: the angle of t turns is 2*pi*t.

    Phase(turns: sympy.Expr)
    Phase.zero()
    Phase.turns(value: Rational | int)          an exact fraction of a turn
    Phase.root_of_unity(index, dim: Dim)        the phase of omega_d^index, index/d turns
    Phase.symbol(name: str)                     a free phase parameter, in turns

    p + q, p - q, -p
    p.to_sympy_turns()
    p.is_zero, p.is_concrete, p.free_symbols
    p.at(index)                                 the phase with INDEX replaced by index
    p.scale(factor: int)
    p.substitute(mapping) -> Phase
    p.to_radians()                              the exact angle 2*pi*t
    p.to_complex() -> complex                   e^{i angle}; requires a concrete phase

A concrete rational number of turns is reduced modulo 1. A symbolic expression is never
reduced: j/d with d symbolic, or a free symbol, is left as it is. Consequently two phases that
differ by a whole number of turns symbolically may compare unequal.

3.3 class PhaseVector

    PhaseVector(dim: Dim, entries: Mapping[int, Phase] | None = None,
                rule: Phase | None = None, expand: bool = True)

The phase data of one spider: a sparse map from index to Phase, implicitly zero elsewhere,
plus an optional rule, a Phase in the symbol INDEX that gives the phase at every index. Sparse
storage is what allows a phase vector at a symbolic dimension to exist.

  - Index 0 is the gauge and must not carry a nonzero entry.
  - At a concrete dimension, indices must lie in 1 .. d-1.
  - At a symbolic dimension a rule is kept as a rule. At a concrete dimension the rule is
    also stored unexpanded but read as expanded entries (entries() lists every nonzero index,
    rule is None), unless expand=False, which leaves the rule visible. get(k) evaluates the
    rule at k on demand (exact rational arithmetic for a polynomial rule with rational
    coefficients); the expanded entries are built on first read and cached. Equality,
    hashing, repr and str use the expanded reading, so a rule-built vector and its
    entry-built twin are equal, hash equal and print alike.
  - substitute returns the vector itself when the mapping names none of its free symbols,
    and substitutes only the entries and weights whose symbols it names; a rule stays
    unexpanded when the dimension is or becomes concrete.

    v.dim, v.length                             length is d - 1, or None when d is symbolic
    v.entries() -> Mapping[int, Phase]          the entries, the rule excluded unless it is
                                                read expanded (see above)
    v.rule -> Phase | None                      None when read expanded
    v.stored_rule -> Phase | None               the rule as stored
    v.get(index) -> Phase                       zero when absent or at index 0
    v.at(index) -> sympy.Expr                   the turns at a possibly symbolic index
    v.is_zero, v.is_concrete, v.free_symbols
    v.substitute(mapping) -> PhaseVector
    v + w                                       entrywise sum; the dimensions must agree

Errors: PhaseDomainError for an index out of range, a gauge violation, mismatched dimensions or
numeric evaluation of a symbolic phase; PhaseGrammarError for inexact input such as a float.

3.4 Complex weights

A PhaseVector may also carry exact complex weights, making it the parameter of a [P1] Z box:
the spider's coefficient at index k is

    a_k = w_k e^{2 pi i alpha_k},     a_0 = 1.

    PhaseVector(dim, entries, rule, expand, weights=None, weight_rule=None)
    PhaseVector.from_weights(dim, weights=None, rule=None)   weights only, no phase

    v.weights() -> Mapping[int, Scalar]         the stored weight entries
    v.weight_rule -> Scalar | None              a Scalar in INDEX
    v.weight(index) -> Scalar                   1 at index 0, else the entry, else the rule,
                                                else 1
    v.coefficient(index) -> Scalar              weight(index) * e^{2 pi i get(index)}
    v.weight_at(index) -> sympy.Expr            the weight at a possibly symbolic index
    v.is_weighted                               some weight differs from 1

An entry overrides the weight rule at its index; an entry equal to the value it would
otherwise take is dropped. At a concrete dimension a weight rule is stored unexpanded and
read as expanded entries (weights(), weight_rule None) unless expand=False, as for the phase
rule. is_zero is False for a weighted vector, so every rule requiring a
phaseless spider refuses it. v + w multiplies weights and adds phases (the fusion of Z boxes).
A vector without weights is stored, compared, hashed and printed exactly as before.


4. SCALARS: archytaszx.algebra.scalar
-------------------------------------

4.1 The model

A Scalar is an exact complex number built from Gaussian rationals, roots of unity
omega_d^j = e^{2*pi*i*j/d} (d and j possibly symbolic), powers of dimensions, algebraic numbers
written CRootOf(p, i) (section 5) and free symbols. Scalars are closed under sum, product, integer power and complex conjugation.

The standing rule of this module is that nothing quotients a global factor: s and 2*s are
unequal, s and omega_d*s are unequal, and there is no method, public or private, that makes
them equal. Comparison up to a global phase is a mode of the numeric oracle only (SEMANTICS.txt,
section 4).

4.2 class Scalar

    Scalar.zero(), Scalar.one()
    Scalar.rational(p, q=1)
    Scalar.gaussian_rational(real, imag)
    Scalar.omega(dim: Dim, index=1)             omega_d^index
    Scalar.from_dim(dim: Dim)                   the factor d
    Scalar.dim_power(dim, numerator, denominator=1)
                                                d^(numerator/denominator), e.g. d^(-1/2)
    Scalar.symbol(name)                         a free scalar parameter
    Scalar.from_phase(phase: Phase)             e^{i angle}
    Scalar.index_sum(dim, body)                 sum_{k=0}^{d-1} body(k), left unevaluated

    a + b, a - b, a * b, a ** n, -a
    s.conjugate()
    s.to_sympy()
    s.is_concrete, s.free_symbols
    s.is_zero, s.is_one                         True only when provable
    s.substitute(mapping) -> Scalar
    s.to_complex() -> complex                   requires a concrete scalar
    s.simplify(*, max_steps=1024, ranges=None) -> Scalar
    s.with_dimension_floors(floors) -> Scalar

4.3 Normalisation and simplification

Construction performs only cheap, always-sound normalisation: collecting like terms, concrete
rational arithmetic, and reducing a root-of-unity index modulo d when d is concrete.

simplify closes index sums. Its central identity is the character sum

    sum_{k=0}^{d-1} omega_d^{j k} = d * [j == 0 mod d]

where the bracket is the exact atom ModDelta(j, d). After closing sums it removes the bound
indices the resulting deltas pin and puts the remaining deltas in echelon form. ranges gives
the extents of free indices. A simplification that exceeds max_steps raises
ScalarBudgetError. With enumerate_concrete=False a sum no identity closes is left as a Sum
instead of being expanded over one concrete index.

Carries at a concrete modulus are kept unsplit. An index u < m pinned by a delta
[c u + r == 0 mod M], m | M and c a unit mod M, whose weight is not m-periodic in u or whose
-c r leaves [0, m), is replaced by the atom ModResidue(-c r, m), the delta kept at that value
when m < M. The atom reads as its argument where only the residue matters: in omega powers
whose coefficient times m is an integer, in deltas [a x + r == 0 mod M] with M | a m, and when
ranges bound the argument in [0, m). A free index v of range m that a delta of the term pins
to the same residue (c v + L with -c L == x mod m) replaces ModResidue(x, m). An echelon pivot
v == value mod m with v of range m and value bounded in [0, m) is substituted everywhere,
deltas of other moduli included; otherwise only where the expression is m-periodic in v.

with_dimension_floors sets to zero every [s | c] whose modulus symbol s has, by the given
floors, a least value above |c|; it is how facts such as "d >= 3" enter a comparison.

4.4 Auxiliary functions

    ModDelta(j, m)     [j == 0 mod m]; evaluates when decidable, otherwise moves j to a
                       canonical residue
    ModGcd(c, m)       gcd(c, m); evaluates when decidable
    ModResidue(x, m)   x mod m in [0, m) for a concrete m; evaluates when x is an integer,
                       otherwise takes x's coefficients into [0, m)

Errors: ScalarDomainError (numeric evaluation of a symbolic scalar, zero denominator, zero to a
negative power), ScalarGrammarError (inexact input), ScalarSumError (a malformed index sum),
ScalarBudgetError (simplification budget exceeded).


5. ALGEBRAIC NUMBERS: archytaszx.algebra.algebraic
--------------------------------------------------

canonical(expr) is the canonical form of an algebraic number without free symbols: a rational;
a Gaussian rational a + b i as CRootOf of its primitive minimal polynomial, index 0 when b < 0
and 1 otherwise; any other number as CRootOf(p, i), p its primitive minimal polynomial with
positive leading coefficient and i the root's index in sympy's root order, found as the unique
root within a third of the least root separation at rising precision. Two algebraic numbers are
equal exactly when their canonical forms are identical. canonical_scalar and canonical_vector
apply it to a Scalar and to every coefficient of a phase vector (giving a weight vector, at a
concrete dimension). NotAlgebraicError (a ValueError) marks free symbols or a value without a
minimal polynomial.

level(n) lists the numbers of level n: rationals of height n (max(|p|, q)), primitive roots of
unity of order n, a + b i with b != 0 and n - 1 the larger height of a and b, and the roots of
irreducible integer polynomials of degree D >= 2 and largest coefficient H with D + H = n. Every
algebraic number has a level; algebraic_numbers() lists each once, by level; up_to_level(K) is
every number of level at most K, as scalars. The exhaustive search over algebraic values
(PROOFS.txt, section 3.3) draws its free values from up_to_level.


6. NUMBER FIELDS: archytaszx.algebra.numfield
---------------------------------------------

A FieldCertificate writes finitely many algebraic numbers over one real algebraic number θ:

    FieldCertificate(poly, lo, hi, atoms)
        poly    the integer coefficients of p, constant term first
        lo, hi  rationals with p(lo) < 0 < p(hi), exactly one root of p in [lo, hi], and every
                Bernstein coefficient of p' on [lo, hi] positive; θ is that root
        atoms   (atom, re, im, rect) per atom: atom = re(θ) + i im(θ), re and im rational
                coefficient tuples (constant first) of degree below deg p, rect a rational
                rectangle (ax, ay, bx, by) holding the atom and one root of its minimal
                polynomial

An atom is a maximal subterm of a closed value that is not a rational, i, a sum, a product or an
integer power: a CRootOf, a radical, a root of unity e^{2 pi i r}, any closed algebraic
subterm.

    atoms(expr) -> list                      the atoms of a closed value
    algebraic(atom) -> bool                  a minimal polynomial and an isolated numeric value
    certificate(values) -> FieldCertificate  a checked certificate for every atom of values
    check(cert)                              NumberFieldError unless every claim holds
    element(cert, expr) -> (re, im)          expr over the atoms, reduced modulo p
    key(cert, expr) -> sympy.Expr            re + i im of expr: equal values, equal keys
    inverse(cert, expr) -> sympy.Expr        the inverse of re + i im, in the field
    derivative(poly), bernstein_derivative(cert)

check uses exact arithmetic only: p irreducible over Q; the sign change, a Sturm count of one
root and positive Bernstein coefficients of p' on [lo, hi]; for each atom, its minimal
polynomial m evaluated at re + i im is 0 modulo p and i^2 = -1; a rational interval enclosure of
re(θ) + i im(θ) over [lo, hi] lies in rect; rect contains sympy's isolating interval of the atom
and exactly one root of m (count_roots); a real atom has im = 0, a rational atom a constant
re. So the root of m in rect is both the atom and re(θ) + i im(θ).

certificate finds a certificate by exact algebra, then checks it. Each real atom is a real
generator with its minimal polynomial; a non-real atom α of minimal polynomial m (degree n)
contributes the real generator re α + c im α, whose polynomial is the resultant over pairs of
roots of m (degree n^2). Generators combine one at a time into θ = θ_old + c g (c from a fixed
list), with the minimal polynomial of θ an irreducible factor of a resultant and g and θ_old
recovered as polynomials in θ from a linear gcd over Q(θ); a non-real atom is recovered from a
linear gcd over Q(θ)(i). Numerics (mpmath, 60 digits) only select factors and roots. The
interval is dyadic, shrunk until p' has positive Bernstein coefficients on it and the rectangles
isolate. Values that are not algebraic (pi, e^{2 pi i r} with r irrational) have no certificate
(NotAlgebraicError).

The kernel inference of PROOFS.txt, section 3.3, compares values by key; the Lean export states
θ through p, lo and hi (LEAN.txt, section 6).
