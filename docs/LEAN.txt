ARCHYTASZX DOCUMENTATION
========================

The Lean 4 Library and Proof Export

Applies to: ArchytasZX 2.0.x
Lean library: archytaszx/lean (Lean 4.35.0, Mathlib)
Exporters: archytaszx.semantics.kernel (lean), lean_net, lean_sym, lean_refute, lean_numeric,
           lean_scalar


1. PURPOSE
----------

The engine's proofs and refutations can be exported as Lean 4 source and checked by Lean,
independently of the engine. The export targets a Lean library, bundled with the package, in
which a diagram is a tensor network over the complex numbers and every rewrite rule is a lemma
proved once. A proof that Lean accepts is therefore correct relative to Lean, Mathlib, the
library's definitions, and the translation of the two diagrams into networks.


2. SETTING UP
-------------

See INSTALL.txt, section 3. In brief, from the library directory:

    lake exe cache get
    lake build
    lake env lean /path/to/proof.lean

From Python, archytaszx.semantics.lean_net.check(source, path) writes the source to path and
runs Lean on it. In the shell:

    :certificate lean proof.lean        write the stored proof to proof.lean
    :certificate lean                   print it instead

In a script file, a relative path resolves against the script's directory.


3. THE SEMANTICS IN LEAN
------------------------

3.1 Networks (ArchytasZX.Net)

A Net lists generator instances whose legs carry wire labels. A label shared by two legs is a
contracted index; int lists the labels summed over and bnd the open boundary labels. eval is
the sum over the internal labels of the product of the generator tensors. An environment Env
gives the dimension of every label, the dimensions of the nodes and their phase vectors.

The main statement is

    Equal E s N s' N'           s * eval N = s' * eval N'

between two scaled networks under one environment: the same tensor, scalars included.

4. PROOFS OF EQUALITY
---------------------

4.1 Rewrite proofs (archytaszx.semantics.lean_net)

A kernel theorem without free multiplicities is exported over the network semantics. Each
diagram becomes a Net: one node per diagram node, legs labelled by wire, boundary slot i
labelled i (outputs first, then inputs). The theorem states Equal between the start and goal
networks with the diagrams' own scalars. Its proof composes, by Equal.trans and Equal.symm:

  - for a rule step: an isomorphism into a context C ++ L, the rule's lemma applied to L,
    wire_elim for every bare wire the rule leaves, and an isomorphism onto the next network;
  - for an isomorphism step: iso, decided by isoOK.

A step of a rule of the complete axiomatisation (COMPLETENESS.txt) is proved by its rule
lemma (section 8); a step of a rule without one is an unproved axiom (section 9, G9).

The scalar identity between the two sides is a linear_combination whose coefficients the
engine computes and Lean's ring checks (archytaszx.semantics.lean_scalar). A sum
Σ_{k<D} e^{2πiθk} (a closed spider whose phase rule is θk) is written as it is,
∑ k ∈ Finset.range D, ex (θ * k), and is an atom of that check. Other scalars outside the exact
model (π, irrational powers, conjugates, other sums) become opaque complex variables named in
the theorem's header.

4.2 Families

A family over bang-box multiplicities is a network whose bundled labels carry a chain of
multiplicities. A rule applied inside node boxes is lifted to every multiplicity by
induction, one box at a time, peeling one copy per step; an induction proof of the engine,
nested ones included, becomes a Lean induction over the same peeling. A port box on Z and X
spiders or on a W node's outputs steps by Equal.of_port.

A diagram of the engine with bang boxes is, in Lean, a Diagram with boxes (Diagram.lean, DBox:
id, multiplicity index, parent box, node scope, port scope; box-free diagrams have none).
Diagram.expand ks (Expand.lean) is the box-free diagram with every box b instantiated at ks b.mult,
the least-id leaf box first: a port box grows each of its boundary legs into k consecutive legs;
a node box makes k copies of its nodes and internal wires, a wire crossing into it becomes k legs
of the outside node, copy j on the j-th, and its boundary ports become one block, copy by copy,
at the place of the first of them; at k = 0 the boundary ports of its enclosing node boxes are
first gathered into one block each. On every boxed diagram of examples/ and the battery, at every
assignment of 0..3, the expansion was checked equal to the engine's instantiate_symbol up to
renaming of nodes, boundary slots and leg positions fixed.

The engine's boundary order after expansion has a closed form, the key order: slot i's copy t
(its copy index along its node boxes, outermost first, then its port boxes) is placed by the
key made of, for each node box of i up to the first whose least slot on i's side is i itself,
that least slot and the copy coordinate in that box, then i, then the remaining node-box
coordinates outermost first, then the port-box coordinates innermost first. Diagram.expandK ks
(OrderK.lean) is the expansion with its boundary listed in key order; it is the meaning a family
statement has. On every boxed diagram of examples/ and the battery, at every assignment of 0..3,
expandK agrees with instantiate_symbol, boundary order included. The key of each slot depends
only on keySig (its least slots and its order of copy coordinates), a list the kernel compares.

The bridge (Bridge, NetDigits, FamBridge, Order, OrderK, FamK) proves, for every multiplicity
assignment, that the family network D.toNetB off at a bundled boundary value equals the expansion
in key order at that value's digits, read in key order. DEqual_expandK_of_checks turns Equal of
the family networks of two boxed diagrams into DEqual of their expansions in key order, given
FamOK of both (the box invariant, node shapes, the family's label dimensions; decided by the
kernel), equal boundary data (bndData: each slot's dimension and chain) and equal keySig;
Equal_of_DEqual_expandK is its converse. Every family export, except those of section 9 (G11),
ends in

    theorem main_diagrams : ∀ params mults, DEqual env s₀ (D0.expandK ks) s₁ (D1.expandK ks)

(¬ ∀ ... for a refutation), D0 and D1 the engine's boxed diagrams as data, with D_net
(toNetB = the exported network) and D_ok (FamOK) proved by decide +kernel. DEqualF E M s₁ D₁ s₂
D₂ states the same at every assignment σ of the multiplicity symbols, the boxes of index i at
M σ i.

4.3 Proofs by the normaliser (archytaszx.semantics.lean_sym)

Verdicts not reached by rewriting (for example by symbolic contraction) are proved by the
verified normaliser of symbolic sums. Both networks are written as symbolic sums: dimensions as
monomials in dimension symbols, phases as roots of unity times atoms ex g, scalars as constant
expressions. equal_sym proves Equal when the normal form of the scaled difference is empty; the
Lean kernel computes that normal form (decide +kernel), for every value of the dimension symbols
and phase parameters. The normaliser removes one bound variable per move:

  - a variable not used;
  - a variable pinned by a delta whose modulus divides its range;
  - a variable read only through roots of unity of its range, by the character sum
        sum_k omega_D^(k A + B) = D [D | A] omega_D^B;
  - a variable read only through one delta of its range, counted by gcd(a, D).

Canonicalisation substitutes each delta's pivot, merges roots of unity, evaluates constants and
sorts factors. A phase stored at index j requires d > j, which the theorem states as a
hypothesis. sym_check in the exporter mirrors the Lean computation to decide whether to export.

4.4 Assumptions

Dimension equalities a rewrite assumed (deferred constraints) become hypotheses of the
theorem; the proof is carried out over the merged network and transported to the diagrams' own
dimensions (Equal.of_mapDim'). main_diagrams then states DEqual over the diagrams at their own
dimensions, with the same hypotheses, when their boundary dimension indices agree; DEqual
compares indices, so for a goal whose boundary carries the other of two dimensions assumed
equal, main_diagrams states DEqual over the diagrams read through the identification of those
dimensions (D.mapDim (dimMap dimIds), as for a wire below), from the merged theorem mainM. A node whose legs differ in dimension, such as d^p and d^q, is
proved under the hypothesis d^p = d^q. The shell reports every such condition.

A wire of the start or goal that joins two dimensions (a diagram that is well formed only when
they are equal) identifies them: the networks are the merged ones, and main_diagrams states
DEqual over the engine's diagrams read through that identification, D.mapDim (dimMap dimIds)
(Diagram.mapDim renames dimension indices); each identified dimension keeps an index of its own
in the literal (from 1000000), and dimIds, commented with the dimensions' names, sends it to
the index it is read as. Families are stated the same way, over (D.mapDim (dimMap dimIds)).expandK.

4.5 Fallback export

A theorem the network export does not cover (free multiplicities outside the cases above,
pending parameters, dangling ports, or a scalar the exporter cannot
write) is exported without imports: an opaque Diagram type, one constant per diagram, one axiom
per rule instance, isomorphism, instantiation, peeling and context the kernel checked, and the
theorem as their composition. This export records the structure of the proof but does not
re-prove its steps; the shell reports it as "opaque diagrams, every step an axiom".


4.6 Mixed theorems

A kernel theorem between diagrams with discards (MIXED.txt) is exported as the equality of
their doubled diagrams, which is the equality of their CPMs by definition (MIXED.txt, 2.3),
through the normaliser of symbolic sums (4.3) when it settles the doubled pair: no axiom
beyond propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. A DOUBLED proof is a pure proof of the
doubled diagrams and exports as such (4.1). Otherwise a mixed theorem takes the fallback
export of 4.5, each discard step an axiom unproved_DISCARD_<rule>_N. The network semantics has
no discard Kind; Mixed.lean states the CPM of a network through its purification, and the
discard rules as tensor identities.


5. REFUTATIONS
--------------

5.1 Exact refutations (archytaszx.semantics.lean_refute)

A verdict that two diagrams differ is exported as

    ¬ ∀ params, Equal ...

proved at the counterexample (or a default sample point). Both networks are evaluated there by
eval_cyc, computed by the Lean kernel, as a product of (√d)⁻¹ factors times a polynomial in
ζ = om M. At one boundary assignment the scaled difference is rewritten, over the relations of
its atoms and of ζ, into a nonzero integer vector of length at most φ(M); cyc_ne shows that
such a vector does not vanish, by the degree of the cyclotomic minimal polynomial. √p for an
odd prime p is a quadratic Gauss sum, checked by its square and the sign of its real part.

5.2 Numeric refutations (archytaszx.semantics.lean_numeric)

A point involving π, an irrational phase or another value outside ℚ(ζ) is refuted by ball
arithmetic (ArchytasZX.Ball): every subterm receives an enclosure, square roots from their
squares, π from its 20-digit bounds, exponentials from a Taylor bound, and a ball that excludes
zero contradicts the equality.

5.3 Interfaces

Diagrams whose interfaces differ are refuted on their leg dimensions. Families whose boundaries
are written differently are refuted after instantiating their multiplicities.


6. WHAT IS TRUSTED
------------------

    #print axioms main_diagrams

(main where an export has no diagram statement) should list only propext, Classical.choice and
Quot.sound. What remains trusted is:

  - Lean and Mathlib;
  - the definitions of the bundled library (the tensors of the generators);
  - the definitions Diagram.eval, Diagram.expand and Diagram.expandK, and the diagram literals
    (D0, D1: nodes, wires, boundary and boxes, as data) that the final theorem main_diagrams
    is stated over; the translation of a diagram into a network is checked by the kernel
    (D_net), so a wrong translation is rejected, not trusted.

Agreement of Diagram.expandK with the engine's instantiate_symbol is checked on examples/ and the
battery, not proved.

A rule step for which no lemma exists, or an isomorphism the exporter cannot match, becomes an
axiom named unproved_* stating that one step over the same semantics. Such axioms are listed in
the file header and counted by the shell ("lean: tensor-network semantics, N unproved
step(s)"). The constructs that currently have no Lean proof path are listed, with the reason
for each, in section 9.

normaliser of section 4.3) at d = 2, 3, 5, at symbolic d where the rule's template builds
there, over the rule's arity classes, and checks every file with lake env lean.


9. GAPS
-------

G1 Binder and splitter under the normaliser. The binder entry [k = a t + b] needs t as a
coefficient and div/mod to eliminate; an X spider or F on an s t wire gives characters
omega_s^(t k l) whose sums depend on gcd(t, s). Normaliser proofs over such networks are not
attempted. By rewriting, every connective rule with an instance has a rule lemma: the
mixed-dimensional Z box of [P2] (2.3.3) has its value lemma mixZ (Weights.lean: every leg's pink
dot and binder or splitter pin its product label to the leg's value, times the square root of
the pink dot's dimension), with mixZc for a Z or weighted Z box and mix2, mix3 for two and three
legs; qfDD, qfBZW, qfDZX, qfTA and qfS1 are built on it, qfE and qfK0 directly.
connective_inverse_converse is proved through its forward rule at composite dimensions (a prime
wire has no factorisation, so no instance). A qf_s1 step that joins every leg (its right side a
scalar) is proved by qfS1c, for weighted and plain (phased) Z boxes. binder_definition with only some of its legs bent (an adapter absorbed into a
neighbouring spider) is proved by bindDefP.

G2 Evaluated quadratic Gauss sums. The simplifier closes sum_{k<d} omega_d^(a k^2 + b k + c)
by the classical evaluation with Gauss's sign and by Landsberg-Schaar reciprocity; neither is in
Mathlib. A proof needing one is accepted only when the sums cancel between the sides. No rule
lemma needs an evaluated Gauss sum: zxw_h1, zxw_pcy, zxw_hd and qf_hd are proved by rewriting
(xDef, zcPcy, zxwHD, qfHD), so the gap concerns only whole-proof normaliser exports.

G3 Phase rules with a symbolic coefficient (theta k) under the normaliser. At a bound index s
the rule contributes ex(theta)^s; summing gives a geometric series, outside the normaliser's
monomials. Rule steps are not affected: rule lemmas read phase rules pointwise, and a closed
spider's sum is written explicitly (section 4.1).

G4 Multiplicity-dependent scalars that are not geometric, with port boxes.

G5 Opaque scalars (pi, roots of non-monomials, algebraic numbers written CRootOf) whose ratio
is not exact. An opaque scalar is a parameter of the export (the theorem holds for every value),
so a step whose proof needs a relation the value satisfies (its minimal polynomial) is an
unproved axiom; steps that do not need one, such as a fusion of two such weights, are proved.

G6 Node boxes meeting the rest at a wire, and port boxes inside port boxes, under the
normaliser.

G7 Deferred dimension constraints in families: a family whose rewrite steps assume dimensions
equal (d^p = d^q, for example) is proved over the merged bundled networks, and main_diagrams is
stated over the diagrams read through the identification ((D.mapDim (dimMap dimIds)).expandK,
as for a wire that joins two dimensions, 4.4), not at the diagrams' own dimensions with the
equality as a hypothesis: that would need Equal.of_mapDim' on bundle indices, which it maps only
for plain ones.

G8 Nodes whose legs differ in dimension (d^p against d^q), in families: covered by the
identification of G7 (the exponent symbols are binders with positivity hypotheses); the
statement is over the diagrams with d^q read as d^p, not at their own dimensions.

G9 Steps outside a rule lemma's form: none known. Every rule of the complete axiomatisation has
a rule lemma; a step its builder does not cover would fall back to an unproved axiom.

G10 Normaliser exports on phased diagrams: a closing identity between roots of unity written in
different forms (1 - I + om 6 against 1 - ex(1/4) + ex(1/6)) that the final ring tactic does
not identify; a symCheck whose decide does not reduce in the kernel for 8 nodes. The scalar
prover of rule builders relates sqrt 2 and sqrt 3 to roots of unity of order divisible by 8 and
12 (rt_sub2, rt_sub3); other square roots of naturals stay independent of the roots of unity.

G11 Exports without a diagram statement: a network whose wire labels are not consecutive keeps
its statement over networks only (no input producing one is known). A box-free derivation whose
assumed equality changes a boundary dimension's index (4.4) ends in a diagram statement with the
assumed-equal dimensions identified (dimIds), beside the network theorem at the diagrams' own
dimensions.

G12 Weighted spiders at a symbolic dimension: the coefficient table Ψ is the weight entries
over the weight rule (a function of the index k), times e^{2πi} of the spider's phases (entries
and phase rule); a weight rule the scalar model cannot write raises NetUnsupported, and steps on
such nodes are unproved axioms. Weighted X spiders are exported as Kind.XW.

G13 Cost of large steps: structural side conditions (among them the isomorphism condition isoOKB
of isoB) are decided in the kernel first (decide +kernel); a step's closing isoB names its target
network and is applied by refine before its condition is decided; exports set maxRecDepth to
100000. zxw_hd, zxw_hd_converse, qf_hd, qf_hd_converse and zxw_vw steps check up to d = 7 (a
zxw_hd step at d = 7 in 185 s; the converse's closing isomorphism takes longest, about 20
minutes at d = 6); larger dimensions were not measured.

G14 Mixed diagrams: the network export has no discard generator; a mixed theorem is proved
through its doubled diagrams (4.6). Mixed.lean does not state F2 (doubling is compositional),
for want of a composition operation on Diagram, nor the tensor identities of discard_cx,
discard_merge and discard_spider for n > 1; a NATIVE certificate is not exported step by step.
