ARCHYTASZX DOCUMENTATION
========================

The Lean 4 Library and Proof Export

Applies to: ArchytasZX 2.0.x
Lean library: archytaszx/lean (Lean 4.35.0, Mathlib)
Exporters: archytaszx.semantics.kernel (lean), lean_net, lean_sym, lean_refute, lean_numeric,
           lean_scalar


1. PURPOSE
----------

The engine's proofs and refutations can be exported as Lean 4 source and checked by Lean,
independently of the engine. The export targets a Lean library, bundled with the package, in
which a diagram is a tensor network over the complex numbers and every rewrite rule is a lemma
proved once. A proof that Lean accepts is therefore correct relative to Lean, Mathlib, the
library's definitions, and the translation of the two diagrams into networks.


2. SETTING UP
-------------

See INSTALL.txt, section 3. In brief, from the library directory:

    lake exe cache get
    lake build
    lake env lean /path/to/proof.lean

From Python, archytaszx.semantics.lean_net.check(source, path) writes the source to path and
runs Lean on it. In the shell:

    :certificate lean proof.lean        write the stored proof to proof.lean
    :certificate lean                   print it instead

In a script file, a relative path resolves against the script's directory.


3. THE SEMANTICS IN LEAN
------------------------

3.1 Networks (ArchytasZX.Net)

A Net lists generator instances whose legs carry wire labels. A label shared by two legs is a
contracted index; int lists the labels summed over and bnd the open boundary labels. eval is
the sum over the internal labels of the product of the generator tensors. An environment Env
gives the dimension of every label, the dimensions of the nodes and their phase vectors.

The main statement is

    Equal E s N s' N'           s * eval N = s' * eval N'

between two scaled networks under one environment: the same tensor, scalars included.

4. PROOFS OF EQUALITY
---------------------

4.1 Rewrite proofs (archytaszx.semantics.lean_net)

A kernel theorem without free multiplicities is exported over the network semantics. Each
diagram becomes a Net: one node per diagram node, legs labelled by wire, boundary slot i
labelled i (outputs first, then inputs). The theorem states Equal between the start and goal
networks with the diagrams' own scalars. Its proof composes, by Equal.trans and Equal.symm:

  - for a rule step: an isomorphism into a context C ++ L, the rule's lemma applied to L,
    wire_elim for every bare wire the rule leaves, and an isomorphism onto the next network;
  - for an isomorphism step: iso, decided by isoOK.

A step of a rule of the complete axiomatisation (COMPLETENESS.txt) is proved by its rule
lemma (section 8); a step of a rule without one is an unproved axiom (section 9, G9).

The scalar identity between the two sides is a linear_combination whose coefficients the
engine computes and Lean's ring checks (archytaszx.semantics.lean_scalar). A sum
Σ_{k<D} e^{2πiθk} (a closed spider whose phase rule is θk) is written as it is,
∑ k ∈ Finset.range D, ex (θ * k), and is an atom of that check. Other scalars outside the exact
model (π, irrational powers, conjugates, other sums) become opaque complex variables named in
the theorem's header.

4.2 Families

A family over bang-box multiplicities is a network whose bundled labels carry a chain of
multiplicities. A rule applied inside node boxes is lifted to every multiplicity by
induction, one box at a time, peeling one copy per step; an induction proof of the engine,
nested ones included, becomes a Lean induction over the same peeling. A port box on Z and X
spiders or on a W node's outputs steps by Equal.of_port.

A diagram of the engine with bang boxes is, in Lean, a Diagram with boxes (Diagram.lean, DBox:
id, multiplicity index, parent box, node scope, port scope; box-free diagrams have none).
Diagram.expand ks (Expand.lean) is the box-free diagram with every box b instantiated at ks b.mult,
the least-id leaf box first: a port box grows each of its boundary legs into k consecutive legs;
a node box makes k copies of its nodes and internal wires, a wire crossing into it becomes k legs
of the outside node, copy j on the j-th, and its boundary ports become one block, copy by copy,
at the place of the first of them; at k = 0 the boundary ports of its enclosing node boxes are
first gathered into one block each. On every boxed diagram of examples/ and the battery, at every
assignment of 0..3, the expansion was checked equal to the engine's instantiate_symbol up to
renaming of nodes, boundary slots and leg positions fixed.

A chain box is, in Lean, a DChain (ChainBox.lean): a DBox and its links (l.1, l.2), copy j's l.1
meeting copy j + 1's l.2; the DBox itself has no link field. Each link port of the template is
wired outside the box, a link port on the boundary through an identity node. expandChain D c k
copies the box k times (copyNodes on the diagram without the link wires) and wires each link:
l.2's outside partner to copy 0's l.2, copy j's l.1 to copy j + 1's l.2, copy k - 1's l.1 to l.1's
outside partner, and at k = 0 the two partners to each other (checked by decide on a one-spider
chain at k = 0 and 2). ChainLink.lean: Equal.dropIdentity removes an identity node from a
summed label a to a boundary label b, a read as b in the rest; expand_two_chainNet gives that
chain's expansion at k = 2 (toNet 10), its open-end identity dropped and its labels renamed onto
the chain scheme (Equal.rename, renameOK by decide), equal to chainNet 100 [5] base copy 2.
ChainEvery.lean: expand_closed gives that chain's expandChain at every k + 1 in closed form
(closedK k: the copies 3 + j in copy order wired output 0 to input 0 between the identities 0
and 2, their second outputs on the boundary before the tail's output). net_closed gives its
network at any wire offset o as netK o k; expand_chainNet gives, for every k and every offset
o >= k + 3 (above the k + 3 boundary slots), that network relabelled by the injective fK o k
equal to chainNet 100 [5] base copy (k + 1).
Periodic.lean: Equal.periodic gives two families equal at every index from the pairs at 0 ..
p - 1 and each side's step from k to k + p (its network at k + p equal to a times its network
at k relabelled by an injective f k shared by both sides), by strong induction.
PeriodicChain.lean: famNet W u tl cp hd bd k is a closed chain family, a fixed end tl on the
link labels u, k copies of cp (copy j raised j levels of W by sh W j, labels below W kept) and
the moving end hd raised k levels; the summed labels are tl's, u off bd, each copy's own and its
links one level up, and hd's. fam_step: from blkNet W u cp hd p (p copies then hd, open on u
and hd's boundary) equal to c times headNet u hd, the family at k + p is c times the family at
k (the block raised k levels by Equal.mapL and plugged in place of the last p copies and hd;
famOK by decide gives the label separations). fam_periodic: with the pairs at 0 .. p - 1 equal
to a fixed network G, the family equals G at every k, the scalars perS tab A p k (table entry k
mod p times A^(k / p)) and perS tab' (A c) p k, by Equal.periodic with the identity relabelling.
lean_periodic.export_periodic writes a kernel.periodic theorem whose index is read by a chain on
one side only: the head side of the chain as tl (nodes on neither side with it; a link whose head
end is the boundary keeps that slot as its u label in bd), the box as cp, the tail side as hd;
the pairs at 0 .. p - 1 and the pair (p copies then the tail side, scalar 1; the tail side,
scalar c^(p / period) from chain_period) proved by path sums (net_body), relabelled onto the
templates found by label search (phase indices of equal vectors first made one), A the scalar's
shift_ratio; at each checked k both sides instantiated are linked and equal under their own
scalars. Refused: a chain on both sides, a copy scalar, a box node wired outside off its links,
a tail end on the boundary, the two ends meeting outside the box, qudits, weights and mixed wire
dimensions. A pair whose sides are both zero is proved by net_body side by side: a side with
scalar zero by zero_mul, a network side by its trace steps to a constraint 1 = 0 then .norm and
PSem.val_of_zeroOK (PathSumNetW.lean: a sum with the constraint [[]] is zero). Boxed
qubitization_chebyshev (p = 2, A = 16, c = -1/16), a chain of S spiders against i^n (p = 4) and
the X(pi) flips chain against (1 + (-1)^n)/2 <0|0> (p = 2, its pair at n = 1 zero) are
Lean-checked at every n, with instances at n = 2, 3.

The engine's boundary order after expansion has a closed form, the key order: slot i's copy t
(its copy index along its node boxes, outermost first, then its port boxes) is placed by the
key made of, for each node box of i up to the first whose least slot on i's side is i itself,
that least slot and the copy coordinate in that box, then i, then the remaining node-box
coordinates outermost first, then the port-box coordinates innermost first. Diagram.expandK ks
(OrderK.lean) is the expansion with its boundary listed in key order; it is the meaning a family
statement has. On every boxed diagram of examples/ and the battery, at every assignment of 0..3,
expandK agrees with instantiate_symbol, boundary order included. The key of each slot depends
only on keySig (its least slots and its order of copy coordinates), a list the kernel compares.

The bridge (Bridge, NetDigits, FamBridge, Order, OrderK, FamK) proves, for every multiplicity
assignment, that the family network D.toNetB off at a bundled boundary value equals the expansion
in key order at that value's digits, read in key order. DEqual_expandK_of_checks turns Equal of
the family networks of two boxed diagrams into DEqual of their expansions in key order, given
FamOK of both (the box invariant, node shapes, the family's label dimensions; decided by the
kernel), equal boundary data (bndData: each slot's dimension and chain) and equal keySig;
Equal_of_DEqual_expandK is its converse. Every family export, except those of section 9 (G11),
ends in

    theorem main_diagrams : ∀ params mults, DEqual env s₀ (D0.expandK ks) s₁ (D1.expandK ks)

(¬ ∀ ... for a refutation), D0 and D1 the engine's boxed diagrams as data, with D_net
(toNetB = the exported network) and D_ok (FamOK) proved by decide +kernel. DEqualF E M s₁ D₁ s₂
D₂ states the same at every assignment σ of the multiplicity symbols, the boxes of index i at
M σ i.

4.3 Proofs by the normaliser (archytaszx.semantics.lean_sym)

Verdicts not reached by rewriting (for example by symbolic contraction) are proved by the
verified normaliser of symbolic sums. Both networks are written as symbolic sums: dimensions as
monomials in dimension symbols, phases as roots of unity times atoms ex g, scalars as constant
expressions. equal_sym proves Equal when the normal form of the scaled difference is empty; the
Lean kernel computes that normal form (decide +kernel), for every value of the dimension symbols
and phase parameters. The normaliser removes one bound variable per move:

  - a variable not used;
  - a variable pinned by a delta whose modulus divides its range;
  - a variable read only through roots of unity of its range, by the character sum
        sum_k omega_D^(k A + B) = D [D | A] omega_D^B;
  - a variable read only through one delta of its range, counted by gcd(a, D).

Canonicalisation substitutes each delta's pivot, merges roots of unity, evaluates constants and
sorts factors. A phase stored at index j requires d > j, which the theorem states as a
hypothesis. sym_check in the exporter mirrors the Lean computation to decide whether to export.

Families are proved by induction on each multiplicity, one copy split off per step
(Equal.of_split_at). A node box meeting only the boundary peels a disjoint copy, plugged beside
the network at k; a port box steps by Equal.of_port_at. A node box whose copies meet one Z
spider at its bundled inputs, the spider having a boundary output, steps by unfusing the split
spider (fusionZG read backwards) so that the network at k, its output renamed to the new wire,
is a region; the induction hypothesis is plugged there (Equal.rename, plug) and the rest is
proved against the other side's split by the normaliser.

4.4 Assumptions

Dimension equations a rewrite assumed (DEFERRED and BOUND constraints) become hypotheses of
the theorem; the proof is carried out over the merged network and transported to the diagrams'
own dimensions (Equal.of_mapDim'). main_diagrams then states DEqual over the diagrams at their own
dimensions, with the same hypotheses, when their boundary dimension indices agree; DEqual
compares indices, so for a goal whose boundary carries the other of two dimensions assumed
equal, main_diagrams states DEqual over the diagrams read through the identification of those
dimensions (D.mapDim (dimMap dimIds), as for a wire below), from the merged theorem mainM. A node whose legs differ in dimension, such as d^p and d^q, is
proved under the hypothesis d^p = d^q. The shell reports every such condition. An assumed
equation that is also the mismatch of a wire of the start or goal, as every BOUND one is, has
no network at the diagrams' own dimensions; the export raises NetUnsupported ("a wire joins
dimensions assumed equal").

A wire of the start or goal that joins two dimensions (a diagram that is well formed only when
they are equal) identifies them: the networks are the merged ones, and main_diagrams states
DEqual over the engine's diagrams read through that identification, D.mapDim (dimMap dimIds)
(Diagram.mapDim renames dimension indices); each identified dimension keeps an index of its own
in the literal (from 1000000), and dimIds, commented with the dimensions' names, sends it to
the index it is read as. Families are stated the same way, over (D.mapDim (dimMap dimIds)).expandK.

4.5 Fallback export

A theorem the network export does not cover (free multiplicities outside the cases above,
pending parameters, dangling ports, or a scalar the exporter cannot
write) is exported without imports: an opaque Diagram type, one constant per diagram, one axiom
per rule instance, isomorphism, instantiation, peeling and context the kernel checked, and the
theorem as their composition. This export records the structure of the proof but does not
re-prove its steps; the shell reports it as "opaque diagrams, every step an axiom".

A symbolic contraction step is the exception when its two contracted diagrams (the whole sides,
or for local_contraction the parts off the two regions, cut ends open) have no bang box and no
free multiplicity and one of the normaliser of symbolic sums (4.3), the qubit path-sum network
proof or the odd-prime one proves them equal. That proof is included in the file as
namespace <N>.C<i> (its theorem C<i>.main, tactic macros local), the file imports ArchytasZX,
the opaque part moves to the namespace ArchytasZX.Opaque, and the step is

    axiom net<i> : (type_of% @<N>.C<i>.main) → ∀ ..., d<a> ... = d<b> ...

applied to C<i>.main: the contraction itself is proved; the axiom states only that the step's
diagrams are the proved networks (plugged into one context for a local contraction). The shell
reports "opaque diagrams, every step an axiom but N symbolic contraction(s) proved over
networks"; the benchmark runner classes the file as opaque.

A symbolic contraction step whose contracted diagrams carry a bang box or a free multiplicity
is proved again by the induction rung of decide (base case decided, step case by its step
ladder, other symbols held at a default sample) on that pair; when every box multiplicity
reading the one free multiplicity n is n + c (c >= 1), on the pair at n = m - c, m fresh. The
induction theorem is stated as a lemma fam<i> (its own contraction steps exported the same way,
two levels deep at most), applied at m = n + c between two instantiation axioms

    axiom inst<j> : ∀ (n : Nat), d<s> (n + c) = d<a> n

and used as the step itself (or, for local_contraction, through net<i> from fam<i>). Boxed
teleportation_chain and repeater_chain (doubled, symbolic d): the contraction axiom becomes an
induction whose step is a box-free local contraction proved as C1.main.

An induction's base case whose two sides carry no free multiplicity is proved as a network
theorem C<i>.main (the symbolic-sum normaliser or the path-sum network proof) carried by one
net<i> axiom, in place of its rewrite-rule instance axioms; the base case of repeater_chain
(doubled, symbolic d) is one net axiom over C1.main. The induction theorems of the rung are
kept per pair (kernel._INDUCTED, the latest 16, failures too) and an INDUCTION decision's
proof is kept when from_decision reads it, so a pair already settled is not decided again
(the repeater_chain export: 58 s the first time, 0.1 s the second in one process).


4.6 Mixed theorems

A kernel theorem between diagrams with discards (MIXED.txt) is exported as the equality of
their doubled diagrams, which is the equality of their CPMs by definition (MIXED.txt, 2.3),
through the normaliser of symbolic sums (4.3) when it settles the doubled pair: no axiom
beyond propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. A DOUBLED proof is a pure proof of the
doubled diagrams and exports as such (4.1). Otherwise a mixed theorem takes the fallback
export of 4.5, each discard step an axiom unproved_DISCARD_<rule>_N. The network semantics has
no discard Kind; Mixed.lean states the CPM of a network through its purification, and the
discard rules as tensor identities.


4.7 Restricted theorems (archytaszx.semantics.lean_restrict)

A kernel theorem specialised from a family proof (PROOFS.txt, section 8, step 1) is exported
as the family theorem exactly as above, followed by a corollary stating the restricted claim
over the same diagrams: the final theorem's binders and statement (main_diagrams where the
export has one, else main) are copied with, per clause, n = v written for n and its binder
dropped, n >= c a hypothesis (h_n_ge : c ≤ n) after the binder of n, n = a*m + c written as
(a * m + c) with m bound in its place, and d = v written for d; a hypothesis binder left
without variables (0 < 5) is discharged by norm_num. The corollary is proved by applying the
final theorem:

    theorem main_restricted (d : ℕ) (n : ℕ) (h_n_ge : 2 ≤ n) (h_d : 0 < d) :
        DEqual ((F d).env (ksOf [n])) ... (D0.expandK (ksOf [n])) ... :=
      main_diagrams d n h_d
    theorem main_at (d : ℕ) (h_d : 0 < d) : DEqual ((F d).env (ksOf [3])) ... :=
      main_diagrams d 3 h_d

(main_at when every clause is n = v or d = v). No translation is involved: the corollary is
about the same networks and diagram literals as the family theorem. The kernel's subst steps
are not exported; the corollary replaces them. The opaque export (4.5) of a theorem with subst
steps states the premise as a lemma over its multiplicities, applies it at the clause's value,
and joins it to the substituted sides by instantiation axioms.

A theorem about the restricted pair (step 3) under one clause n >= c or n = a*m + c (not
n = m) is stated over the original diagrams. The kernel's shift (kernel.shift) rebuilds both peeled
sides by restrict.apply_clause and checks them against the theorem's sides; the export splits
each original family network c times along the box of n (Net.split at the offsets H, 2H, 4H,
...), identifies the c-fold split with the peeled network by isoB, applies the restricted
pair's proof at a*k (the restricted pair's boxes of multiplicity a*k read on the original box's
multiplicity index), and lifts it by Equal.of_split_at c times:

    theorem main (d : ℕ) (h_d : 0 < d) (k : ℕ) :
        Equal ((F d).env (ksOf [(k + 2)])) ... N0 ... N1
    theorem main_diagrams : ∀ (d : ℕ) (h_d : 0 < d) (k : ℕ),
        DEqual ((F d).env (ksOf [(k + 2)])) ... (D0.expandK (ksOf [(k + 2)])) ...
    theorem main_restricted (d : ℕ) (h_d : 0 < d) (n : ℕ) (h_n_ge : 2 ≤ n) :
        DEqual ((F d).env (ksOf [n])) ... (D0.expandK (ksOf [n])) ... := by
      obtain ⟨k, rfl⟩ : ∃ k, n = k + 2 := ⟨n - 2, by omega⟩
      exact main_diagrams d h_d k

D0 and D1 are the original boxed diagrams. For a > 1 the statements are at (a * k + c) and
main_restricted binds k. The normaliser's export (4.3) does the same on the split networks.

Several multiplicity clauses, or clauses n = c (kernel.lift, the inference shift at scale 0,
each clause recorded as (n, a, c, m), a = 0 for n = c): along each clause's multiplicity in
turn, both original family networks are split c times, and zeroed (Net.zero) at n = c; the
result is identified with the restricted pair by isoB, and the proof is lifted by
Equal.of_split_at and Equal.of_zero_at, one per step. A clause n >= c or n = a*m + c keeps a
step binder (nk for n, last, in clause order) and its value (nk + c), (a * nk) or
((a * nk) + c); a clause n = c its value. main and main_diagrams are stated at these values,
over the original boxed diagrams:

    theorem main_diagrams : ∀ (d : ℕ) (h_d : 0 < d),
        DEqual ((F d).env (ksOf [1, 2])) ... (D0.expandK (ksOf [1, 2])) ...
    theorem main_diagrams : ∀ (d : ℕ) (h_d : 0 < d) (nk : ℕ) (pk : ℕ),
        DEqual ((F d).env (ksOf [(2 * nk), (pk + 1)])) ... (D0.expandK ...) ...

main_restricted (lean_restrict.shift_corollary) then binds n with c ≤ n in place of nk for each
clause n >= c, each step binder found by its value in the statement; a clause n = a*m + c with
a > 1 keeps nk. A box at n = c is peeled before the boxes nested in it: its copies are split
off and the box zeroed, then each nested box is split in every copy at once, the split offsets
starting above every label of both networks and every offset, the zero steps' included,
registered in the label table. A box nested in a box under n >= c or n = a*m + c is not lifted.
A side without boxes is stated through expandK too when the other side's boundary lies outside
every box. The normaliser's export (4.3) lifts the same way along clauses n = c and n >= c
(a = 1).

Clauses d = v: the export states its final theorem over the restricted diagrams, then
main_diagrams_original over the same diagram literals read with the original tables: Δo, Φo
and Ψo are the dimension, phase and weight tables of the original diagrams with d free, at the
indices of the export's tables (each node of the restricted literal matched to the original
node of the same id); Δo_at, Φo_at and Ψo_at prove them equal to Δ, Φ and Ψ at d = v, and
Eo_at (Fo_at for families) the environments:

    theorem main_diagrams_original (d : ℕ) (h_d_eq : d = 5) :
        DEqual (Eo d) 1 D0 1 D1 := by
      subst h_d_eq
      simp only [Eo_at]
      exact main_diagrams

Clauses d = v combine with the multiplicity clauses above (the d clauses substituted first),
and main_restricted is then stated over main_diagrams_original. Other restricted-pair
theorems (a box nested in a box under n >= c or n = a*m + c; two original dimensions or
phases on one index, or identified dimensions, under d = v; a single clause n = m; a clause
n = a*m + c with a > 1 under the normaliser; a theorem the network export does not cover) are
exported by the exporters above, unchanged, with the restriction, the substitution and the
note "UNCERTIFIED for the original diagrams" (kernel.UNLINKED) in the header comment; their
statement is about the restricted diagrams only, and the shell reports "lean: uncertified for
the original claim". A restriction of floors d >= c alone substitutes nothing and carries no
such note.

A normaliser proof of a restricted pair under one clause n >= c (or n = m + c), c >= 1, is
replaced by the normaliser's proof over the original diagrams: the family lemma of both
networks split c times along the box of n, lifted by Equal.of_split_at, stated at n + c, with
the corollary main_restricted over n (c ≤ n). A symbolic-contraction EQUAL that needed a floor d >= c is exported by the normaliser (4.3)
with the floor among the dimension floors, the hypothesis (h_d : c ≤ d) of every theorem; a
refutation under a floor is refuted at its in-floor point unchanged; a rewriting proof has no
floor hypothesis (it holds for every d). An instance decided on its expanded pair (n = v) is a
box-free theorem; its link to the boxed original at v is the expandK item of section 6.

5. REFUTATIONS
--------------

5.1 Exact refutations (archytaszx.semantics.lean_refute)

A verdict that two diagrams differ is exported as

    ¬ ∀ params, Equal ...

proved at the counterexample (or a default sample point). Both networks are evaluated there by
eval_cyc, computed by the Lean kernel, as a product of (√d)⁻¹ factors times a polynomial in
ζ = om M. At one boundary assignment the scaled difference is rewritten, over the relations of
its atoms and of ζ, into a nonzero integer vector of length at most φ(M); cyc_ne shows that
such a vector does not vanish, by the degree of the cyclotomic minimal polynomial. √p for an
odd prime p is a quadratic Gauss sum, checked by its square and the sign of its real part.

The points tried are the counterexample, then the default sample points ordered by the sum of
the absolute values of their coordinates, smallest first (lean_refute._by_size), at most 16; a
point whose evaluation needs a factor table above 200000 entries, or whose factor tables
together hold more than 200000 cyclotomic terms, or whose field ℚ(ζ) has φ(M) above 512, is
skipped, so a large
counterexample is replaced by the smallest sample point that still separates. When no point
gives an exact refutation, the same points are tried by ball arithmetic (5.2). Boundary
assignments are enumerated lazily, at most 4096 per point.

5.2 Numeric refutations (archytaszx.semantics.lean_numeric)

A point involving π, an irrational phase or another value outside ℚ(ζ) is refuted by ball
arithmetic (ArchytasZX.Ball): every subterm receives an enclosure, square roots from their
squares, π from its 20-digit bounds, exponentials from a Taylor bound, and a ball that excludes
zero contradicts the equality. A negative integer power of a positive constant is enclosed
from the enclosure of its base (nearR_inv), so 1/π and π⁻² have balls.

5.3 Interfaces

Diagrams whose interfaces differ are refuted on their leg dimensions. Families whose boundaries
are written differently are refuted after instantiating their multiplicities.

5.4 Verdicts without a refutation

decide_equal reports UNEQUAL from the oracle, a symbolic witness or induction only with its
Lean refutation (Decision.refutation, decide.lean_refutation, over the doubled diagrams when
either side has a discard); without one the verdict is UNEQUAL_UNCERTIFIED and its reason
ends in "uncertified: no Lean refutation (...)". prove reports an oracle REFUTED only with its
Lean refutation (ProofOutcome.refutation), otherwise NOT_FOUND ("not refuted: no Lean
refutation"). The shell prints such a counterexample as "uncertified counterexample", induct
appends "not reported refuted: no Lean refutation", and certificate refuses to fall back to the
session derivation after a decide that built no certificate. The oracle's check of a
normal-form verdict (rung 2) and of a proof's two halves is not a certificate; the certificate
of such a verdict is the kernel theorem of section 4.1. A rung 1 (interface) verdict, and an
oracle mismatch in the numbers of boundary outputs and inputs (which DEqual, comparing the
boundary dimensions outputs then inputs, does not see), are exact and keep their verdict; the
export of the first is that of 5.3.

Points with no refutation: a scalar or phase with a transcendental constant other than π and
its integer powers (E, exp of a non-rational multiple of π I, for example), and points every
one of which needs a factor table above the bound.


6. WHAT IS TRUSTED
------------------

    #print axioms ArchytasZX.Export.main_diagrams

(main where an export has no diagram statement; network, normaliser and refutation exports
declare their theorems in the namespace ArchytasZX.Export, the opaque export of 4.5 in
ArchytasZX) should list only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. What remains trusted is:

  - Lean and Mathlib;
  - the definitions of the bundled library (the tensors of the generators);
  - the definitions Diagram.eval, Diagram.expand and Diagram.expandK, and the diagram literals
    (D0, D1: nodes, wires, boundary and boxes, as data) that the final theorem main_diagrams
    is stated over; the translation of a diagram into a network is checked by the kernel
    (D_net), so a wrong translation is rejected, not trusted.

Algebraic scalars (CRootOf values, roots of non-monomials, sums and quotients of them) are
exported over one real number θ, the primitive element of a number-field certificate
(archytaszx.algebra.numfield, ALGEBRA.txt section 6): an irreducible integer polynomial θp and a
dyadic interval (θlo, θhi). Every algebraic value is written qa(θ) + I qb(θ), qa and qb rational
polynomials. The kernel inference algebraic (PROOFS.txt 3.3) checks that both sides of a meet
have the same coefficients in this form and that each atom's form is a root of its minimal
polynomial inside its own isolating rectangle. In Lean, θ is a binder of every theorem with the
hypotheses

    (hθ : pev θp θ = 0) (hθlo : θlo < θ) (hθhi : θ < θhi)

and the file proves

    theorem θ_isolated : ∃! x, θlo < x ∧ x < θhi ∧ pev θp x = 0

by root_unique (the sign of θp at θlo and θhi by norm_num, the derivative positive on the
interval by its Bernstein coefficients, an identity checked by ring). The hypotheses therefore
name exactly one real number, and every step uses only the relation pev θp θ = 0
(linear_combination); which atom each form stands for is stated in the file header and is not
part of the theorem. Nothing about the certificate is trusted outside Lean: a wrong polynomial,
form or interval makes a step or θ_isolated fail to check.

Agreement of Diagram.expandK with the engine's instantiate_symbol is checked on examples/ and the
battery, not proved. A restricted-pair theorem exported without a statement over the original
diagrams (section 4.7) is not a certificate of the restricted claim: its header carries the
note "UNCERTIFIED for the original diagrams" and the shell reports it as uncertified; an export
with main_diagrams over the original boxed diagrams, or with main_diagrams_original, is.

A rule step for which no lemma exists, or an isomorphism the exporter cannot match, becomes an
axiom named unproved_* stating that one step over the same semantics. Such axioms are listed in
the file header and counted by the shell ("lean: tensor-network semantics, N unproved
step(s)"). The constructs that currently have no Lean proof path are listed, with the reason
for each, in section 9.

A step of a rule without a builder is proved through its reverse rule (lean_net._through_forward;
the forward rule of a converse, the converse of a rule): the reverse rule is applied to the
step's result, then identity_removal and spider_fusion steps consuming only nodes absent from
the step's start, found by depth-first search, reach a diagram matched to that start by an
isomorphism or an algebraic meet, and the composite is reversed by Equal.symm; those tidying
steps alone are tried first. qf_d1_converse,
zxw_va_converse, qf_va_converse, zxw_kz_converse, qf_hd_converse, zxw_hd_converse,
w_z_effect_converse and wz_bialgebra_converse have builders of their own (LocalEq.symm' or
LocalEq.flip' of the forward lemma). Converses with free values (zxw_s1, zxw_ad, zxw_ept,
scalar_box, x_fusion) are proved for the values the step records.

The acceptance check exports single steps of every rule on the step route (never the
normaliser of section 4.3) at d = 2, 3, 5, at symbolic d where the rule's template builds
there, over the rule's arity classes, and checks every file with lake env lean.


9. GAPS
-------

G1 Binder and splitter under the normaliser. The binder entry [k = a t + b] needs t as a
coefficient and div/mod to eliminate; an X spider or F on an s t wire gives characters
omega_s^(t k l) whose sums depend on gcd(t, s). Normaliser proofs over such networks are not
attempted. By rewriting, every connective rule with an instance has a rule lemma: the
mixed-dimensional Z box of [P2] (2.3.3) has its value lemma mixZ (Weights.lean: every leg's pink
dot and binder or splitter pin its product label to the leg's value, times the square root of
the pink dot's dimension), with mixZc for a Z or weighted Z box and mix2, mix3 for two and three
legs; qfDD, qfBZW, qfDZX, qfTA and qfS1 are built on it, qfE and qfK0 directly.
connective_inverse_converse is proved through its forward rule at composite dimensions (a prime
wire has no factorisation, so no instance). A qf_s1 step that joins every leg (its right side a
scalar) is proved by qfS1c, for weighted and plain (phased) Z boxes. binder_definition with only some of its legs bent (an adapter absorbed into a
neighbouring spider) is proved by bindDefP.

G2 Evaluated quadratic Gauss sums. The simplifier closes sum_{k<d} omega_d^(a k^2 + b k + c)
by the classical evaluation with Gauss's sign and by Landsberg-Schaar reciprocity; neither is in
Mathlib. A proof needing one is accepted only when the sums cancel between the sides. No rule
lemma needs an evaluated Gauss sum: zxw_h1, zxw_pcy, zxw_hd and qf_hd are proved by rewriting
(xDef, zcPcy, zxwHD, qfHD), so the gap concerns only whole-proof normaliser exports.

G3 Phase rules with a symbolic coefficient (theta k) under the normaliser. At a bound index s
the rule contributes ex(theta)^s; summing gives a geometric series, outside the normaliser's
monomials. Rule steps are not affected: rule lemmas read phase rules pointwise, and a closed
spider's sum is written explicitly (section 4.1).

G4 Multiplicity-dependent scalars that are not geometric, with port boxes.

G5 Transcendental and oversized scalars. Algebraic values are exported over the number-field
certificate of section 6. A value that is not algebraic (pi, E, exp of a non-rational multiple of
pi I) stays a parameter of the export (the theorem holds for every value of it), so a step whose
proof needs a relation between such values is an unproved axiom. The cost of an algebraic export
grows with the degree of θ: for a non-real root of x^5 - x - 1 (degree 20) the export takes 144 s
and the Lean check 576 s.

G6 Under the normaliser: node boxes meeting the rest at a wire other than at the bundled
inputs of one Z spider with a boundary output, and port boxes inside port boxes.

G7 Deferred dimension constraints in families: a family whose rewrite steps assume dimensions
equal (d^p = d^q, for example) is proved over the merged bundled networks, and main_diagrams is
stated over the diagrams read through the identification ((D.mapDim (dimMap dimIds)).expandK,
as for a wire that joins two dimensions, 4.4), not at the diagrams' own dimensions with the
equality as a hypothesis: that would need Equal.of_mapDim' on bundle indices, which it maps only
for plain ones.

G8 Nodes whose legs differ in dimension (d^p against d^q), in families: covered by the
identification of G7 (the exponent symbols are binders with positivity hypotheses); the
statement is over the diagrams with d^q read as d^p, not at their own dimensions.

G9 Steps outside a rule lemma's form: none known. Every rule of the complete axiomatisation has
a rule lemma; a step its builder does not cover would fall back to an unproved axiom.

G10 Normaliser exports on phased diagrams: a closing identity between roots of unity written in
different forms (1 - I + om 6 against 1 - ex(1/4) + ex(1/6)) that the final ring tactic does
not identify; a symCheck whose decide does not reduce in the kernel for 8 nodes. The scalar
prover of rule builders relates sqrt 2 and sqrt 3 to roots of unity of order divisible by 8 and
12 (rt_sub2, rt_sub3), sqrt 5 to those of order divisible by 5 (rt_sub5), and sqrt p for every
other odd prime p to those of order divisible by p through the quadratic Gauss sum with its sign
(GaussSign.lean: gauss_sign, gauss_rt; I^h written as (-1)^(h/2) for even h = (p-1)/2, else
through I or a root of unity of order divisible by 4); other square roots of naturals stay
independent of the roots of unity.

G11 Exports without a diagram statement: a network whose wire labels are not consecutive keeps
its statement over networks only (no input producing one is known). A box-free derivation whose
assumed equality changes a boundary dimension's index (4.4) ends in a diagram statement with the
assumed-equal dimensions identified (dimIds), beside the network theorem at the diagrams' own
dimensions.

G12 Weighted spiders with bundled legs. Weighted Z and X spiders (Kind.ZW, Kind.XW) are exported at
symbolic dimensions with their weight rule as a function of the index k : ℕ, and in families
through Fam.coef (a box multiplicity n inside a weight or phase is the parameter table_n of the
tables, so main_diagrams holds for every table_n and n); Iverson atoms ModDelta and gcd atoms ModGcd of k are written as Lean terms. A
weighted spider inside a port box (one leg bundling k legs) has no network kind: the bundled
kinds ZB and XB carry phases only, so its export raises NetUnsupported "a weighted spider with
bundled legs". The engine has no proof of such an induction either (state copy refuses a
weighted spider). A weight rule the scalar model cannot write still raises NetUnsupported.

G13 Cost of large steps: structural side conditions (among them the isomorphism condition isoOKB
of isoB) are decided in the kernel first (decide +kernel); a step's closing isoB names its target
network and is applied by refine before its condition is decided; exports set maxRecDepth to
100000 and maxHeartbeats to 2000000. zxw_hd, zxw_hd_converse, qf_hd, qf_hd_converse, zxw_vw and
zxw_vw_converse steps check up to d = 8 (at d = 8 on a cluster node: zxw_hd 298 s, zxw_hd_converse
351 s; at the default heartbeat limit both d = 8 steps end in a heartbeat timeout); larger
dimensions were not measured.

G14 Mixed diagrams: the network export has no discard generator; a mixed theorem is proved
through its doubled diagrams (4.6). The conjugation of MIXED.txt 2.1 is not stated per
generator in Lean; cpm_conj states its effect on any network whose entries are conjugated. Mixed.lean does not state F2 (doubling is compositional),
for want of a composition operation on Diagram, nor the tensor identities of discard_cx,
discard_merge and discard_spider for n > 1; a NATIVE certificate is not exported step by step.

G15 Path-sum proofs. export_proof and export_net write AData for amplitude atoms (Amplitude atoms, section 4),
but decline atoms beside a Boolean elimination that leaves a support or differs between the
sides, an atom not on (1 + y) g or y g at an [Average], and pairs whose reduced sums end on different atoms (a unit-root atom that the
canonical form folds into the phase on one side only). There are no qudit
semantics ([Gauss] and the Legendre form of G(a) included), so export_proof declines traces
using them. export_net covers Z, X, F, B and S on dimensions 2^k (legless and weighted
spiders included) and W on dimension 2 but not a wire on two boundary ports; it raises for that and the path_sum EQUAL
stays EQUAL_UNCERTIFIED. PathSum.lean, PathSumSem.lean, PathSumRules.lean, PathSumNet.lean and
PathSumDigits.lean check (no sorry) and are in the ArchytasZX root import list.
