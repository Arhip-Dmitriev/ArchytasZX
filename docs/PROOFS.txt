ARCHYTASZX DOCUMENTATION
========================

Layer D (part 2): Deciding and Proving Equality

Applies to: ArchytasZX 2.0.x
Modules: archytaszx.semantics.decide, certify_decision, prove, induction, induction_kernel,
         certificate, kernel, restrict


1. THE QUESTION AND THE ANSWERS
-------------------------------

The central question is whether two diagrams denote the same linear map, scalar included, for
every value of their free symbols: dimensions, phases, scalars and multiplicities. In this
generality the question is undecidable, so every procedure below is sound and incomplete:

  - EQUAL is reported only with evidence that holds for every value: a replayable derivation,
    a symbolic contraction with every symbol formal, or an induction whose base and step are
    both settled, and with a proof the kernel re-checks (Decision.proof).
  - UNEQUAL is reported only with a refutation: interfaces that can never agree, or an oracle
    counterexample at an assignment that satisfies every symbol's assumptions or a symbolic
    witness entry, either with its Lean refutation (LEAN.txt, section 5.4).
  - EQUAL_UNCERTIFIED and UNEQUAL_UNCERTIFIED carry the same evidence as EQUAL and UNEQUAL
    without the proof or Lean refutation; their reason ends in "uncertified: no proof the
    kernel re-checks (...)" or "uncertified: no Lean refutation (...)".
  - Every other outcome is UNKNOWN, with the reason the procedure stopped.

Equations that a derivation assumed rather than verified (DEFERRED and BOUND dimension
constraints, one per equation up to orientation) are reported with the verdict as its assumptions; the verdict is conditional on them.


2. DECIDING EQUALITY: archytaszx.semantics.decide
-------------------------------------------------

    decide_equal(left, right, *, rules=None, guard=DEFAULT_GUARD, samples=None,
                 max_samples=24, use_symbolic=True, use_induction=True, max_depth=2,
                 tolerance=1e-9, max_elements=DEFAULT_MAX_ELEMENTS, use_saturation=True,
                 saturation_limits=DECIDE_SATURATION_LIMITS, mixed=False,
                 restrict=None, describe=None, prove_equal=True) -> Decision

prove_equal=False skips the proof of an EQUAL verdict: EQUAL is kept and Decision.proof is
None. The proof is built by archytaszx.semantics.certify_decision.decision_proof: the
decision's own certificates, else the normaliser of symbolic sums, else prove; a restricted
decision through the family's proof or the restricted pair's. describe gives the diagram
comments of the Lean refutation and the normaliser proof.

With restrict (a Restriction, clause text or a mapping, section 8) the decision is the
restricted decision of section 8; without clauses it is the decision below.

2.1 The ladder

decide_equal tries the following rungs in order and stops at the first that settles:

  1. INTERFACE              The numbers of boundary inputs or outputs differ, or a pair of
                            boundary dimensions has no solution: UNEQUAL. A boundary port
                            under bang boxes counts as the product of their multiplicities,
                            so each count is a polynomial in the free multiplicities; counts
                            that differ as polynomials are UNEQUAL with the first point of
                            the grid {0..e_1} x ... x {0..e_k} (e_i the degree of the
                            difference in the i-th multiplicity) where they differ and both
                            sides instantiate, named in the reason. With a port under a bang
                            box and equal counts, per-position dimensions are not compared.
  2. NORMAL_FORM            Both diagrams are reduced to normal form (REWRITE.txt, section 6).
                            When the normal forms agree and rung 3 finds no mismatch: EQUAL.
  3. ORACLE_COUNTEREXAMPLE  The numeric oracle evaluates both diagrams at the sample grid; a
                            mismatch above tolerance (relative to the larger entry, at least 1)
                            is UNEQUAL. A sample whose tensors exceed max_elements (default:
                            the process's memory limit / 16 B / 4, SEMANTICS.txt section 4) is
                            refused, the reason naming the cap and its source. Samples run in
                            increasing planned size (the larger boundary-dimension product of
                            the two sides instantiated at the sample); once one has evaluated, a
                            sample planning more than 16 times the largest evaluated size is
                            refused as "larger than needed". The sample grid is unchanged.
  4. SATURATION             Both diagrams are added to one e-graph, saturated within
                            saturation_limits; when their classes merge: EQUAL. Each edge of
                            the explaining path yields a certificate that must replay.
  5. SYMBOLIC_CONTRACTION   Both diagrams are contracted with every symbol formal; equal
                            tensors are EQUAL. Otherwise SYMBOLIC_WITNESS: an entry at a sample
                            assignment and an index tuple where the two differ is UNEQUAL.
                            When the comparison leaves a nonzero entry difference (also under
                            identical replicated factors), the witness search also tries each
                            sample with every dimension symbol raised to one above the
                            difference's largest integer root (root_points), and the oracle
                            evaluates those points too; an oracle mismatch there without a
                            witness is UNEQUAL as ORACLE_COUNTEREXAMPLE.
  5b. PATH_SUM              When both diagrams translate to path sums (every dimension a
                            concrete power of two, generators Z, X, F, B, S; SEMANTICS.txt,
                            section 5.2): equal canonical forms are EQUAL; different canonical
                            forms are UNEQUAL as SYMBOLIC_WITNESS at the boundary index where
                            the entries differ; otherwise the reason names the path variables
                            left and the ladder goes on. use_path_sum=False turns it off. An
                            EQUAL by path_sum carries no kernel proof of its own: the proof is
                            sought as for any EQUAL (section 6), else EQUAL_UNCERTIFIED; the
                            Lean export of the reduction trace (lean_pathsum) is not written.
  6. INDUCTION              A base case decided recursively plus a step case proved on one
                            multiplicity index: EQUAL. Induction proves EQUAL only from base 0.
                            The step is tried left to right, then right to left; nesting is
                            bounded by max_depth. A step case settled by uniform rewrite or
                            induction rewrite gives the verdict a kernel theorem
                            (Decision.induction_proof, section 6); one settled by symbolic
                            contraction gives none.

When either side has a discard, or mixed=True, the ladder runs unchanged on the two doubled
diagrams and the method is DOUBLED, the settling rung in Decision.inner_method and the doubled
pair in Decision.doubled (MIXED.txt, section 5). Exception: when a node-scope bang box of a
doubled side owns boundary slots but has no sheets flag, or its slots do not split evenly
between the plain and conjugate halves of a boundary list, the doubled ladder is withheld.
Only the oracle runs, on the original pair (each sample expanded, then doubled): a mismatch
is UNEQUAL by ORACLE_COUNTEREXAMPLE, otherwise UNKNOWN, reason "doubled comparison withheld:
...", stops (doubled, withheld).

2.3 The sample grid

    sample_grid(left, right, floors=None) -> tuple[assignment, ...]

The default assignments range over every free symbol of both diagrams:

  Symbol kind      Values, in order
  ---------------  ---------------------------------------------
  dimension        2, 3, 4, 1
  multiplicity     0, 1, 2, 3
  phase            1/3, 1/4, 0, 1/2
  integer          1, 2, 0, 3, -1, 4, 5, -2, 6, 7, -3, 8
  rational         the phase values, then 1, 2, -1/3, -1/4, -1, -1/2, -2

Diagonal assignments come first, then the remaining tuples by largest index, index sum and
tuple. Only values satisfying each symbol's sympy assumptions are used. A sample at which a
diagram is outside its domain (for example a phase index above d - 1) is refused and counted;
the summary line reports how many samples were evaluated and refused, and the first refusal.
A dimension named in floors samples the first four values at least max(floor, its phase-vector
floor) of 2, 3, 4, 1, 5, 6, ...

    interface_reason(left, right) -> str | None              rung 1 alone

    refute_by_symbolic_witness(left, right, *, samples=None, ...) -> EntryWitness | None
    dimension_floors(*diagrams) -> dict[str, int]
            the least value of each dimension symbol for which every phase vector over it is
            defined (one above its largest stored index)


3. PROOF SEARCH: archytaszx.semantics.prove
-------------------------------------------

    prove(start, goal, *, moves=None, limits=DEFAULT_SEARCH_LIMITS, bidirectional=True,
          cache=None, refute=True, samples=None, max_samples=24, tolerance=1e-9,
          max_elements=DEFAULT_MAX_ELEMENTS, induction_depth=2, restrict=None) -> ProofOutcome

prove settles a goal in this order:

  1. Interfaces that can never agree: REFUTED.
  2. With refute, an oracle counterexample: REFUTED with its Lean refutation
     (ProofOutcome.refutation); NOT_FOUND when it has none.
  3. Bidirectional tactic search (REWRITE.txt, section 8). A path found is certified and
     checked: PROVED, or CHECK_FAILED if the check fails.
  4. When search finds no path and a multiplicity is free, induction: the base case at 0 is
     proved the same way; for the step, one side's peeled successor at k + 1 is searched until
     a union of its regions is that side at k; the hypothesis replaces it by the other side at
     k; the result is proved equal to the other side's peeled successor.
  5. Otherwise: NOT_FOUND.

    ProofStatus: PROVED | REFUTED | NOT_FOUND | CHECK_FAILED
    ProofOutcome(status, reason, certificate, check, search, counterexample, assumptions,
                 induction, mixed, restriction, family, family_holds, restricted_pair,
                 counterexample_original, refutation)

With restrict, prove and prove_exhaustive run the three steps of section 8
(restricted_outcome): the unrestricted proof first, kept when PROVED or REFUTED inside the
restriction, otherwise the proof of the restricted pair.

3.1 Proof certificates

A ProofCertificate holds two step-sequence certificates: forward from start and backward from
goal, with the tactic names of their moves.

    check_proof(certificate, *, samples=None, max_samples=24, tolerance=1e-9, ...)
        -> ProofCheck

check_proof verifies, stopping at the first failure: (a) start and goal pass validation and
each half begins at its end diagram, id for id; (b) each half replays; (c) the two replayed
diagrams have isomorphic comparison views; (d) the oracle finds no counterexample between
start and goal. An InductionProof is checked piece by piece, then the oracle is run on its two
sides.

3.2 Exhaustive search

    prove_exhaustive(start, goal, *, refute=True, samples=None, max_samples=24,
                     tolerance=1e-9, max_elements=DEFAULT_MAX_ELEMENTS, on_layer=None,
                     algebraic=False, restrict=None) -> ProofOutcome

prove_exhaustive runs steps 1 and 2 of prove, then archytaszx.rewrite.exhaustive
.exhaustive_search: a breadth-first search from start and from goal at once over every rule
of RULES, automatic or not, converses included. Each layer applies every match of every rule
to every diagram of the previous layer, a rule with candidate values for its free variables also
at each candidate (COMPLETENESS.txt, section 6); a diagram whose comparison view is isomorphic to one
already reached on its side is pruned. Layer k of the start side is expanded before layer k of
the goal side, and both before layer k + 1, so a derivation of n enumerated steps is found by
layer n of the start side, and by layer ceil(n/2) when its second half uses rules with
converses (every rule but 2.22 and 2.28 has one). A meeting gives an ordinary ProofCertificate, checked as
prove's are. There is no depth cap and no time limit; when both sides close without meeting
the outcome is NOT_FOUND. COMPLETENESS.txt, section 6, states for which inputs it terminates.
In the shell: prove exhaustive [against] [TARGET].

3.3 Search over algebraic values

    prove_exhaustive(start, goal, ..., algebraic=True) -> ProofOutcome

runs archytaszx.rewrite.exhaustive.algebraic_search instead: rounds K = 1, 2, ..., each a search
of the kind of 3.2 from both sides to depth ceil(K/2), in which every free weight-vector variable of a
rule (the splits and insertions of the converses) also takes every vector whose entries are
algebraic numbers of level at most K (archytaszx.algebra.algebraic: rationals by height, roots
of unity by order, Gaussian rationals, then roots of irreducible integer polynomials by degree
plus largest coefficient). Where a rule's conditions determine the other free variables from the
first (zxw_s1_converse and x_fusion_converse: b = c / a coefficientwise when no coefficient of a
is 0; zxw_ad_converse: b = c - a), they are computed instead of enumerated. Diagrams are compared by algebraic_view (normal_form): every spider
parameter as weights and the scalar, each value in canonical form (a rational or CRootOf of its
minimal polynomial), so equal values compare equal. Both diagrams need concrete dimensions and
algebraic parameters (ProveGrammarError otherwise). Each round is finite; the rounds are not
bounded. COMPLETENESS.txt, section 6, states the termination theorem. In the shell: prove
exhaustive algebraic [against] [TARGET].

The kernel checks such a meet with the inference kernel.algebraic(a, b, cert): a node renaming
carries a onto b with every spider coefficient and the scalar equal in the number field of a
number-field certificate cert (ALGEBRA.txt, section 6), built from the values when not given and
checked either way. Each value is reduced to its pair (re, im) of polynomials in the field's
generator modulo p; values are compared by these pairs, never by sympy's comparison of CRootOf
forms. The theorem is a = a' (a with b's values; Inference "algebraic", data the certificate)
followed by iso(a', b). kernel.check of a proof certificate uses it at a meet that iso rejects,
and reports iso's error when both fail. The Lean export proves the step from p(θ) = 0
(LEAN.txt, section 4.1).


3.4 Mixed operands

prove on mixed operands first searches with the discard rules (tactics.mixed_moves) and returns
a ProofOutcome whose mixed field is a MixedCertificate of kind NATIVE; otherwise, and in
prove_exhaustive, it proves the doubled pair and returns kind DOUBLED (both doubled diagrams
with node origins and the inner pure proof). check_mixed(certificate) re-doubles a DOUBLED
certificate's sides id for id and checks the inner proof. The kernel refuses a pure rule's
step that consumes a discard; on mixed diagrams a Theorem states equality of CPMs and its iso
compares mixed scalars by modulus. MIXED.txt, section 5.


4. INDUCTION OVER MULTIPLICITIES: archytaszx.semantics.induction
----------------------------------------------------------------

    prove_by_induction(left, right, *, index=None, base=None, witness={},
                       ladder=DEFAULT_LADDER, rules=None, step_symbol="k", width=4,
                       max_steps=64, certify_proof=True, mode=EXACT, tolerance=1e-9,
                       max_elements=DEFAULT_MAX_ELEMENTS, restrict=None) -> InductionResult

proves an equality between two families for every value of one multiplicity symbol, index
(by default the lexicographically least free multiplicity). Every other symbol is either held
symbolic or fixed at the value witness gives it, for the tiers that need concrete values.
With restrict, the three steps of section 8: a clause n = v fixes a held symbol at v (the
index may not have one), a held value must lie inside its clause, and step 3 inducts the
restricted pair over the fresh symbol m of the index's clause from 0, so induction under
n >= c starts at n = c. InductionResult gains restriction, family, family_holds,
restricted_pair and counterexample_original.

4.1 The obligation

build_obligation constructs six diagrams: both sides at the base value (0 when both evaluate
there, otherwise 1), both sides at the step symbol k (the hypothesis), and both sides at k + 1
(the successor).

4.2 The step tiers

The base case is compared by the oracle. The step case is then discharged by the tiers of the
ladder, in order, until one settles:

  Tier                  Method                                                Verdict
  --------------------  ----------------------------------------------------  ----------------
  UNIFORM_REWRITE       rewrite the hypothesis diagrams into each other with  PROVED_UNIFORM
                        the multiplicity never instantiated
  INDUCTION_REWRITE     rewrite the successor diagrams into each other with   PROVED_INDUCTION
                        the hypothesis available as a rule; otherwise peel
                        one copy off each successor, discharge the residual
                        by the hypothesis and compare the peeled copies by
                        symbolic contraction
  SYMBOLIC_CONTRACTION  contract both successors with every symbol formal     PROVED_INDUCTION
  ORACLE_WINDOW         compare by the oracle at every multiplicity in a      SCHEMA_CHECKED
                        window of width values

    Verdict: PROVED_UNIFORM | PROVED_INDUCTION | SCHEMA_CHECKED | REFUTED | INCONCLUSIVE

PROVED_UNIFORM and PROVED_INDUCTION are proofs for every value of the index. SCHEMA_CHECKED is
a finite check and is reported as such, never as a proof. REFUTED carries the counterexample
value of the index.

    InductionResult.verdict, index, base_value, reason, obligation, witness, base, tiers,
                    comparison, counterexample, derivation
    InductionResult.proved, discharge, held_symbolic

held_symbolic names the symbols the settling tier left universally quantified.

4.3 Building blocks

    choose_index, choose_base_value, build_obligation, discharge_base
    hypothesis_diagram, successor_diagram, rename_multiplicity
    hypothesis_rule(obligation)          the hypothesis as a rewrite rule, when matchable
    find_hypothesis_region, replace_region, connected_regions
    discharge_uniform_rewrite, discharge_induction_rewrite, discharge_peeled_hypothesis,
    discharge_symbolic_contraction, discharge_oracle_window


5. CERTIFICATES: archytaszx.semantics.certificate
-------------------------------------------------

5.1 Structure

A Derivation is one node of a proof tree: an initial diagram, a final diagram and the evidence
between them. A STEP_SEQUENCE derivation carries RewriteSteps; an INDUCTION derivation carries
those over a free multiplicity, a base child, a step child and an InductionClaim (the symbol,
the base value, the symbols held symbolic, the tier that discharged the step). A Certificate
pairs a derivation with its CheckMethod: NUMERIC_ORACLE or INDUCTION.

    certify(initial, results, *, label="", check_method=NUMERIC_ORACLE) -> Certificate
    certify_induction(initial, final, results, base, step, claim, *, label="", ...)

5.2 Replay and verification

    replay(certificate, source=None, *, rediscover=True) -> ReplayResult
    verify(certificate, assignment, *, mode=EXACT, tolerance=1e-9, ...)
        -> VerificationReport

replay re-applies every recorded step to a fresh copy of the initial diagram and checks, per
step, that the rule still exists by name, that the consumed nodes and wires are present, that
the rule's pattern verifies the recorded match (Pattern.verify: by default the matcher
rediscovers it; a template rule re-checks it with its own free values), and that the step
apply produces equals the
recorded step; finally that the diagram reached is identical, id for id, to the recorded final
diagram. verify runs replay and then compares the two ends with the oracle at the assignment.

5.3 What a verified certificate establishes

  - The recorded steps re-derive the recorded final diagram exactly.
  - Every rule named still exists.
  - Every match is still found in the diagram it was recorded against.
  - Every recorded side-condition outcome and dimension constraint is what the matcher
    derives afresh.
  - For a NUMERIC_ORACLE certificate, the two ends denote the same tensor at the assignment.

It establishes nothing about other assignments, about the satisfiability of an assumed
dimension equation, or about a rule whose meaning changed under a name it kept. For an
INDUCTION certificate, the checked parts are the base case and one concrete instance of the
step; the step schema itself is discharged by the induction module and is not recorded in
re-checkable form. Tampering with any recorded field makes replay fail.


6. THE PROOF KERNEL: archytaszx.semantics.kernel
------------------------------------------------

The kernel is a small set of inference rules. A Theorem states lhs = rhs for diagrams, every
free multiplicity universally quantified over the naturals, under listed assumptions. Only
these functions construct a Theorem:

  refl(d)                            d = d
  rule(d, step)                      d = d' by re-applying one recorded step at a match the
                                     matcher rediscovers in d
  iso(a, b)                          a = b when their comparison views are isomorphic
  symm(t)                            rhs = lhs
  trans(a, b)                        a.lhs = b.rhs when a.rhs and b.lhs are identical
  induction(start, goal, index, step_symbol, base, side, expose, region, step)
                                     start = goal for every value of index, from a base
                                     theorem at 0, a theorem exposing one side's peeled
                                     successor at k + 1 as a context around that side at k,
                                     and a theorem from that context around the other side at
                                     k to the other peeled successor
  subst(t, clause, lhs=None, rhs=None)
                                     lhs' = rhs' from t and one clause n = v, n = a*m + c
                                     (m fresh for t) or d = v: both sides recomputed from t's
                                     by restrict.apply_clause (section 8.3) and compared id for
                                     id with lhs and rhs when stated
  shift(t, left, right, clause)      left = right at n = m + c for every m, from t about both
                                     sides with the clause n >= c substituted (apply_clause),
                                     matched id for id or by isomorphic comparison views; its
                                     domain is the clause (used by the Lean export, LEAN.txt
                                     section 4.7)
  restricted(t, left, right, restriction, used=())
                                     t read as the restricted claim of left = right: t's sides
                                     must be the restricted pair or isomorphic to it; the floors
                                     in used become the assumptions "c <= d"

    chain(start, steps) -> Theorem   start = d_n through every step
    check(artefact) -> Theorem       the theorem a certificate, proof certificate, induction
                                     proof or RestrictedProof establishes; KernelError at the
                                     first piece that does not check
    RestrictedProof(proof, restriction, left, right, family, floors_used)
                                     a family proof (family=True), checked and specialised by
                                     one subst per substituting clause, or a proof of the
                                     restricted pair, checked and read by restricted
    restricted_symbolic(proof) -> Symbolic
                                     the same for a normaliser proof
    from_decision(decision, left, right) -> Theorem
                                     from a NORMAL_FORM or SATURATION decision's certificates,
                                     or an INDUCTION decision's induction_proof
    symbolic(left, right) -> Symbolic
                                     left = right for every value of the dimensions and
                                     phases, by the Lean normaliser of symbolic sums
    refutation(decision, left, right) -> Refutation
                                     an UNEQUAL decision's counterexample as a refutation
    lean(theorem | refutation | symbolic, name="main", ...) -> str
                                     Lean 4 source (LEAN.txt)

Theorem.size() is the number of inferences (a subst counts one), Theorem.axioms() the rule
names of every rule inference in proof order (subst passes them through), and
Theorem.variables() the free multiplicities of both sides (m, not n, after n = a*m + c).
Theorem.domain holds the clauses that produced a restricted theorem, empty by default, and
Theorem.origin the unrestricted pair. Theorem.statement() is the printed claim: "for all n",
or per clause "for all n >= c (n = m + c)", "for all n = a*m + c (m >= 0)", "at n = v", then
"at d = v", "for d >= c" when the floor is an assumption "c <= d", and "for every d" when the
proof did not use the floor. An
induction certificate of the induction module carries no re-checkable step and is refused by
check; the shell then re-derives a re-checkable proof by search.

The induction rung of decide_equal builds Decision.induction_proof itself
(archytaszx.semantics.induction_kernel), its reason ending in "; kernel proof built" or
"; no kernel proof: <why>":

  uniform rewrite       the tier's steps re-applied to the left side, each at the match of
                        its rule that consumes the same nodes: a chain of rule, trans and iso
                        inferences, with no induction
  induction rewrite     an induction theorem: the base from the base decision's certificates
                        (from_decision), else a prove() search; the tier's peeled successor as
                        the exposed diagram (refl, or iso to the kernel's); its residual as the
                        region; the step from a derivation between the two peeled copies
                        (their normal forms meeting by iso, else a prove() search) re-applied
                        inside both successors
  symbolic contraction  none; the Lean side has no induction over multiplicities for a
                        symbolic step

When no theorem is built the verdict's proof comes from the later builders of Decision.proof
(the normaliser, then search) as before.


7. TRUST MODEL
--------------

From least to most independent of the engine:

  1. A verdict of decide_equal or prove: trusts the engine's rewriting, matching and
     contraction code.
  2. A replayed certificate: trusts the rule definitions and the matcher, but re-derives every
     step.
  3. A kernel theorem: trusts the kernel's inference rules and the rule implementations it
     re-applies.
  4. A Lean proof over the tensor-network semantics: trusted are only Lean, Mathlib, the
     bundled library's definitions, and the translation of a diagram into a network. Every rule
     is a lemma proved in Lean; any step without one is an explicit axiom named unproved_*.

The oracle at every level is evidence at the points it evaluates, never a proof.


8. RESTRICTED DECISIONS: archytaszx.semantics.restrict
------------------------------------------------------

8.1 Clauses

A restriction is a set of clauses, at most one per symbol:

  Clause         Symbol kind     The set of values the claim covers
  -------------  --------------  ------------------------------------------------------
  n = v          multiplicity    {v}, v a non-negative integer literal
  n >= c         multiplicity    {c, c+1, ...}, c a non-negative integer literal
  n = a*m + c    multiplicity    {a*m + c : m >= 0}, a >= 1 and c >= 0 integer literals,
                                 m a fresh symbol (free in neither diagram, not used by
                                 another clause)
  d = v          dimension       {v}, v a positive integer literal
  d >= c         dimension       {c, c+1, ...}, c a positive integer literal

Symbols without a clause keep their domains (multiplicities >= 0, dimensions >= 1). n >= 0
and d >= 1 are accepted and equal no clause. A clause on a symbol free in neither diagram, a
second clause on one symbol, a non-fresh or reused m, a = 0, a negative literal, a dimension
clause other than d = v and d >= c, a dimension literal below 1, and a clause n = v or d = v
whose symbol a side binds to another value are DecideGrammarError naming the clause
(ERRORS.txt).

    parse_restriction(text | mapping | Restriction | None) -> Restriction
    validate(restriction, left, right) -> Restriction     kinds resolved, n >= c given its
                                                          fresh m

8.2 Meaning

left =_R right means: for every assignment of the free symbols in their domains under R, the
two denotations are equal, scalar included. UNEQUAL under R means there is such an assignment
where they differ. A verdict printed for R never claims anything outside R; a stronger fact
found on the way (step 1 below) is printed as its own line.

8.3 The restricted pair

subst_R(D) is D with every clause substituted in order:

  1. n = v: every box carrying n instantiated at v (instantiate_symbol), n in the scalar and
     elsewhere set to v;
  2. n = a*m + c (n >= c is m + c): n replaced by a*m + c in every box multiplicity carrying
     it and in the scalar (n elsewhere is an error); every box whose multiplicity is concrete
     expanded, and every box whose multiplicity keeps a constant term c >= 1 peeled by peel_one
     until it has none, least box id first;
  3. d = v: Diagram.substitute({d: v}), so d is a literal and the concrete-only axioms match it;
  4. d >= c: nothing; the floor is recorded on the restriction.

The parameter environments lose the bindings of substituted symbols.

8.4 The three steps

decide_equal(left, right, restrict=R) runs:

  1. Family. The unrestricted decision on (left, right), floors ignored. EQUAL is EQUAL
     under R: family_holds is True, and the proof is the family proof specialised by subst.
  2. Counterexample in range. When step 1 is UNEQUAL with a counterexample inside R:
     UNEQUAL under R with that counterexample. An INTERFACE verdict is re-checked on the
     restricted pair and kept when it still holds there.
  3. Restricted pair. Otherwise the ladder on (subst_R(left), subst_R(right)), the sample
     grid, the symbolic witness search and the symbolic comparison cut to the floors of R.
     EQUAL is a theorem about the restricted pair; UNEQUAL carries a counterexample over m and,
     in counterexample_original, the same point in n = a*m + c.

prove exhaustive runs steps 1 and 3 side by side, in two threads stopped at their next layer
once the other decides: the family's PROVED or in-range REFUTED wins, otherwise the restricted
pair's outcome (an unprovable family claim never ends its search). samples_checked counts both steps. Without clauses the decision is the unrestricted one,
field for field. Step 1 never uses a concrete-only axiom (its dimensions stay symbolic); they
match in step 3, where d = v is a literal.

8.5 Dimension floors

A clause d >= c raises the floor of d in the sample grid of step 3 (section 2.3) and is passed
to the symbolic rung: the witness search uses only in-floor assignments, and the contractions
are compared first without the floor, then under it. An EQUAL reached only under the floor
records it in Decision.floors_used and is exported with the hypothesis c <= d (LEAN.txt,
section 4.7). Rewriting rungs are unchanged. Floors and phase-vector floors combine by maximum.

8.6 Instances

A restriction binding every free multiplicity (n = v) yields a bang-box-free restricted pair.
With every dimension concrete too, the pair lies in the regime of COMPLETENESS.txt section 1,
and prove exhaustive algebraic under that restriction carries the termination theorem of
COMPLETENESS.txt section 6 for it.
