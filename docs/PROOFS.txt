ARCHYTASZX DOCUMENTATION
========================

Layer D (part 2): Deciding and Proving Equality

Applies to: ArchytasZX 2.0.x
Modules: archytaszx.semantics.decide, certify_decision, prove, induction, induction_kernel,
         certificate, kernel, restrict


1. THE QUESTION AND THE ANSWERS
-------------------------------

The central question is whether two diagrams denote the same linear map, scalar included, for
every value of their free symbols: dimensions, phases, scalars and multiplicities. In this
generality the question is undecidable, so every procedure below is sound and incomplete:

  - EQUAL is reported only with evidence that holds for every value: a replayable derivation,
    a symbolic contraction with every symbol formal, or an induction whose base and step are
    both settled, and with a proof the kernel re-checks (Decision.proof).
  - UNEQUAL is reported only with a refutation: interfaces that can never agree, or an oracle
    counterexample at an assignment that satisfies every symbol's assumptions or a symbolic
    witness entry, either with its Lean refutation (LEAN.txt, section 5.4). The evidence of
    each is rechecked in Python first (section 2.2).
  - EQUAL_UNCERTIFIED and UNEQUAL_UNCERTIFIED carry the same evidence as EQUAL and UNEQUAL
    without the proof or Lean refutation; their reason ends in "uncertified: no proof the
    kernel re-checks (...)", "uncertified: no Lean refutation (...)" or "uncertified:
    interface recheck failed: ...".
  - Every other outcome is UNKNOWN, with the reason the procedure stopped.

Equations that a derivation assumed rather than verified (DEFERRED and BOUND dimension
constraints, one per equation up to orientation) are reported with the verdict as its assumptions; the verdict is conditional on them.
Each verdict states the assumptions of the derivations behind it only: a normal-form match the
normal forms', an induction EQUAL its own (the base case and the step's rewrites; the normal
forms' too when it inducts on them; the nested decisions' of the periodic, carry-open and
chain-end routes; none for the column-count and per-copy routes), an UNKNOWN none.


2. DECIDING EQUALITY: archytaszx.semantics.decide
-------------------------------------------------

    decide_equal(left, right, *, rules=None, guard=DEFAULT_GUARD, samples=None,
                 max_samples=24, use_symbolic=True, use_induction=True, max_depth=2,
                 tolerance=1e-9, max_elements=DEFAULT_MAX_ELEMENTS, use_saturation=True,
                 saturation_limits=DECIDE_SATURATION_LIMITS, mixed=False,
                 restrict=None, describe=None, prove_equal=True) -> Decision

prove_equal=False skips the proof of an EQUAL verdict: EQUAL is kept and Decision.proof is
None. The proof is built by archytaszx.semantics.certify_decision.decision_proof: the
decision's own certificates, else the normaliser of symbolic sums, else prove; a restricted
decision through the family's proof or the restricted pair's. A symbolic-contraction
verdict on a pair with no parameter and no free symbol (scalars, box multiplicities and copy
scalars, leg dimensions, phases) takes the kernel's contraction theorem first and the
normaliser only when the kernel refuses; with a free symbol, and on the doubled pair of a
restricted decision proved over its restricted pair, the normaliser comes first.
describe gives the diagram
comments of the Lean refutation and the normaliser proof.

With restrict (a Restriction, clause text or a mapping, section 8) the decision is the
restricted decision of section 8; without clauses it is the decision below.

2.1 The ladder

decide_equal tries the following rungs in order and stops at the first that settles:

  1. INTERFACE              The numbers of boundary inputs or outputs differ, or a pair of
                            boundary dimensions has no solution: UNEQUAL. A boundary port
                            under bang boxes counts as the product of their multiplicities,
                            so each count is a polynomial in the free multiplicities; counts
                            that differ as polynomials are UNEQUAL with the first point of
                            the grid {0..e_1} x ... x {0..e_k} (e_i the degree of the
                            difference in the i-th multiplicity) where they differ and both
                            sides instantiate, named in the reason. With a port under a bang
                            box and equal counts, per-position dimensions are not compared.
  2. NORMAL_FORM            Both diagrams are reduced to normal form (REWRITE.txt, section 6).
                            When the normal forms agree and rung 3 finds no mismatch: EQUAL.
  3. ORACLE_COUNTEREXAMPLE  The numeric oracle evaluates both diagrams at the sample grid; a
                            mismatch above tolerance (relative to the larger entry, at least 1)
                            is UNEQUAL. A sample whose tensors exceed max_elements (default:
                            the process's memory limit / 16 B / 4, SEMANTICS.txt section 4) is
                            refused, the reason naming the cap and its source. Samples run in
                            increasing planned size (the larger boundary-dimension product of
                            the two sides instantiated at the sample); once one has evaluated, a
                            sample planning more than 16 times the largest evaluated size is
                            refused as "larger than needed". The sample grid is unchanged.
                            A sample planning more than 2^20 elements is deferred: it runs only
                            when every later rung has failed (just before UNKNOWN), or before a
                            NORMAL_FORM EQUAL that no smaller sample cross-checked; a mismatch
                            there is UNEQUAL by ORACLE_COUNTEREXAMPLE, stop (oracle_deferred, ...).
  4. SATURATION             Both diagrams are added to one e-graph, saturated within
                            saturation_limits; when their classes merge: EQUAL. Each edge of
                            the explaining path yields a certificate that must replay.
  5. SYMBOLIC_CONTRACTION   Both diagrams are contracted with every symbol formal; equal
                            tensors are EQUAL. Otherwise SYMBOLIC_WITNESS: an entry at a sample
                            assignment and an index tuple where the two differ is UNEQUAL.
                            When the comparison leaves a nonzero entry difference (also under
                            identical replicated factors, also one still carrying an index
                            sum or divisibility atom), the witness search also tries each
                            sample with every dimension symbol raised to one above the
                            difference's largest integer root, then to the next value that
                            does not divide every nonzero constant term of the ModDelta
                            arguments modulo that symbol (root_points), and the oracle
                            evaluates those points too; an oracle mismatch there without a
                            witness is UNEQUAL as ORACLE_COUNTEREXAMPLE.
  5b. PATH_SUM              When both diagrams translate to path sums (every dimension a
                            concrete power of two, generators Z, X, F, B, S; SEMANTICS.txt,
                            section 5.2): equal canonical forms are EQUAL; different canonical
                            forms are UNEQUAL as SYMBOLIC_WITNESS at the boundary index where
                            the entries differ; otherwise the reason names the path variables
                            left and the ladder goes on. use_path_sum=False turns it off. A
                            pair on dimensions 1, 2, 3, 6 with a 2 and a 3 factor
                            (pathsum_mixed.is_mixed_pair) takes this rung first, right after
                            the interface check; only EQUAL or UNEQUAL there ends the ladder.
                            With
                            enumerate_residual=True (--tgateenum) a pair left with differing
                            residual forms is evaluated exactly: each entry's residual sum over
                            its 2^k path assignments, or p^k over Z_p for an odd-prime sum (k at
                            most max_residual_paths, default 6, refused above it with the
                            reason), at boundary points in increasing Hamming weight (nonzero
                            values 1..p-1 over Z_p; at most 64); the first difference is UNEQUAL as
                            SYMBOLIC_WITNESS, stop path_sum.residual_enum. It never gives EQUAL.
                            Off by default. An
                            EQUAL by path_sum carries no kernel proof of its own: the proof is
                            sought as for any EQUAL (section 6), else EQUAL_UNCERTIFIED; the
                            Lean export of the reduction trace (lean_pathsum) is not written.
  6. INDUCTION              A base case decided recursively plus a step case proved on one
                            multiplicity index: EQUAL. Induction proves EQUAL only from base 0.
                            The step is tried left to right, then right to left; nesting is
                            bounded by max_depth. A step case settled by uniform rewrite,
                            induction rewrite, symbolic contraction, chain_window or
                            tree_window gives the verdict a kernel theorem when its pieces
                            check (Decision.induction_proof, section 6). When neither
                            direction settles, the rung tries the carry left open after a
                            chain (carry_open.open_carry, section 4.4): the open pair by this
                            rung, then the closed pair by decide_equal without induction; then
                            the chain ends cut on both sides (carry_open.open_chain_ends,
                            section 4.4); then the periodic test (section 4.5). When the rung
                            leaves the pair unsettled and either side's normal form took a
                            step, the rung runs once more on the two normal forms (reason
                            "on the normal forms: ..."); only an EQUAL is kept, its kernel
                            theorem the left normal-form certificate, the induction theorem
                            between the normal forms and the reversed right normal-form
                            certificate joined by trans and iso.

When either side has a discard, or mixed=True, the ladder runs unchanged on the two doubled
diagrams and the method is DOUBLED, the settling rung in Decision.inner_method and the doubled
pair in Decision.doubled (MIXED.txt, section 5). Exception: when a node-scope bang box of a
doubled side owns boundary slots but has no sheets flag, or its slots do not split evenly
between the plain and conjugate halves of a boundary list, the doubled ladder is withheld.
Only the oracle runs, on the original pair (each sample expanded, then doubled): a mismatch
is UNEQUAL by ORACLE_COUNTEREXAMPLE, otherwise UNKNOWN, reason "doubled comparison withheld:
...", stops (doubled, withheld).

    Decision.method           DecisionMethod: INTERFACE, NORMAL_FORM, SATURATION,

    Decision.assumptions      assumed dimension equations the verdict rests on
    Decision.samples_checked  oracle samples evaluated
    Decision.parameters_agree whether the two parameter environments agree
    Decision.restriction      the restriction of a restricted decision (section 8), or None
    Decision.family           the unrestricted decision of step 1 (section 8), or None
    Decision.family_holds     True when that decision was EQUAL
    Decision.restricted_pair  the restricted pair a step 3 decision was made on
    Decision.counterexample_original
                              the counterexample in the original symbols (n = a*m + c)
    Decision.floors_used      the dimension floors a symbolic contraction needed
    Decision.induction_reversed
                              True when Decision.induction ran with right as its left side
    Decision.decided          True for EQUAL and UNEQUAL
    Decision.holds_uncertified
                              True for EQUAL_UNCERTIFIED and UNEQUAL_UNCERTIFIED
    Decision.claims_equal     True for EQUAL and EQUAL_UNCERTIFIED
    Decision.claims_unequal   True for UNEQUAL and UNEQUAL_UNCERTIFIED
    Decision.left_nf, right_nf, comparison, induction, saturation

The parameter environment never enters a verdict; parameters_agree reports it separately.

An UNEQUAL verdict keeps its certification only when its evidence passes a Python recheck
at its one point (archytaszx.semantics.refute_check):

    residue refutation     recheck_residue: a fresh _Refuter at the same point, order M,
                           prime p and root w (p prime, w of order exactly M mod p, checked)
                           tabulates each network's leaf tables at the found boundary
                           assignment σ (node residues res(node(n, σ)) shared with the
                           search's _Refuter when both have the same M, phase, coefficient
                           and dimension tables), eliminates them, and confirms that
                           the residues r0, r1 are the search's and f0(w) r0 != f1(w) r1 mod p;
                           a boundary-size refutation recounts the slots
    ball refutation        recheck_numeric: a fresh ball refuter recomputes both entries at σ
                           through the contraction pinned at σ (the search contracts unpinned),
                           checks they are the search's groups, and that the ball (x, y, r)
                           excludes 0 and holds the difference evaluated at 60 digits
    INTERFACE verdict      recheck_interface: the input or the output leg dimensions, each
                           slot repeated by its box multiplicities, differ at a sample point
    interface refutation   none added: its source compares the two leg lists computed from the
                           diagrams at the point directly, and Lean re-decides the comparison

A residue or ball refutation whose recheck fails is no refutation: export_refutation tries the
next sample point and route, and when none passes raises the failed recheck, so the verdict is
UNEQUAL_UNCERTIFIED with stop (refutation, recheck_failed) and reason "uncertified: no Lean
refutation (residue recheck failed: ...)". A failed interface recheck gives
UNEQUAL_UNCERTIFIED with the same stop and reason "uncertified: interface recheck failed:
...". The residue or ball refutation of a pair with a discard is rechecked on the doubled
pair it refutes; a DOUBLED INTERFACE verdict on the original pair. Both refuters walk the
boundary assignments in product order one tuple at a time (lean_refute._odometer), never
materialising a boundary axis, so a leg of dimension 30^8 costs nothing until it is read.
A phase table that is a polynomial in the digits of its index (every entry checked,
digit_poly) is read by the residue refuter and its recheck through the formula at single
indices, so its node's dimension is not bounded by 200000 (LEAN.txt, 5.1).

2.3 The sample grid

    sample_grid(left, right, floors=None) -> tuple[assignment, ...]

The default assignments range over every free symbol of both diagrams:

  Symbol kind      Values, in order
  ---------------  ---------------------------------------------
  dimension        2, 3, 4, 1
  multiplicity     0, 1, 2, 3
  phase            1/3, 1/4, 0, 1/2
  integer          1, 2, 0, 3, -1, 4, 5, -2, 6, 7, -3, 8
  rational         the phase values, then 1, 2, -1/3, -1/4, -1, -1/2, -2

Diagonal assignments come first, then the remaining tuples by largest index, index sum and
tuple. Only values satisfying each symbol's sympy assumptions are used. A sample at which a
diagram is outside its domain (for example a phase index above d - 1) is refused and counted;
the summary line reports how many samples were evaluated and refused, and the first refusal.
A dimension named in floors samples the first four values at least max(floor, its phase-vector
floor) of 2, 3, 4, 1, 5, 6, ...
A multiplicity n on which a node phase (a vector entry or rule, in turns) is a polynomial of
degree D > 3 also gets the samples n = 4, ..., D after the grid, every other symbol at its
first value: an integer-valued polynomial of degree D vanishing mod 1 at n = 0, ..., D vanishes
mod 1 at every n. incrementer/main! (the phase k n (n-1) (n-2) (n-3) / 48 on each output, first
nonzero at n = 4): unknown (induction_reversed.step_not_discharged) -> UNEQUAL at n = 4,
certified, 0.4 s.

3. PROOF SEARCH: archytaszx.semantics.prove
-------------------------------------------

    prove(start, goal, *, moves=None, limits=DEFAULT_SEARCH_LIMITS, bidirectional=True,
          cache=None, refute=True, samples=None, max_samples=24, tolerance=1e-9,
          max_elements=DEFAULT_MAX_ELEMENTS, induction_depth=2, restrict=None) -> ProofOutcome

prove settles a goal in this order:

  1. Interfaces that can never agree: REFUTED.
  2. With refute, an oracle counterexample: REFUTED with its Lean refutation
     (ProofOutcome.refutation); NOT_FOUND when it has none.
  3. Bidirectional tactic search (REWRITE.txt, section 8). A path found is certified and
     checked: PROVED, or CHECK_FAILED if the check fails.
  4. When search finds no path and a multiplicity is free, induction: the base case at 0 is
     proved the same way; for the step, one side's peeled successor at k + 1 is searched until
     a union of its regions is that side at k; the hypothesis replaces it by the other side at
     k; the result is proved equal to the other side's peeled successor.
  5. Otherwise: NOT_FOUND.

    ProofStatus: PROVED | REFUTED | NOT_FOUND | CHECK_FAILED
    ProofOutcome(status, reason, certificate, check, search, counterexample, assumptions,
                 induction, mixed, restriction, family, family_holds, restricted_pair,
                 counterexample_original, refutation)

With restrict, prove and prove_exhaustive run the three steps of section 8
(restricted_outcome): the unrestricted proof first, kept when PROVED or REFUTED inside the
restriction, otherwise the proof of the restricted pair.

3.1 Proof certificates

A ProofCertificate holds two step-sequence certificates: forward from start and backward from
goal, with the tactic names of their moves.

    check_proof(certificate, *, samples=None, max_samples=24, tolerance=1e-9, ...)
        -> ProofCheck

check_proof verifies, stopping at the first failure: (a) start and goal pass validation and
each half begins at its end diagram, id for id; (b) each half replays; (c) the two replayed
diagrams have isomorphic comparison views; (d) the oracle finds no counterexample between
start and goal. An InductionProof is checked piece by piece, then the oracle is run on its two
sides.

3.2 Exhaustive search

    prove_exhaustive(start, goal, *, refute=True, samples=None, max_samples=24,
                     tolerance=1e-9, max_elements=DEFAULT_MAX_ELEMENTS, on_layer=None,
                     algebraic=False, restrict=None) -> ProofOutcome

prove_exhaustive runs steps 1 and 2 of prove, then archytaszx.rewrite.exhaustive
.exhaustive_search: a breadth-first search from start and from goal at once over every rule
of RULES, automatic or not, converses included. Each layer applies every match of every rule
to every diagram of the previous layer, a rule with candidate values for its free variables also
at each candidate (COMPLETENESS.txt, section 6); a diagram whose comparison view is isomorphic to one
already reached on its side is pruned. Layer k of the start side is expanded before layer k of
the goal side, and both before layer k + 1, so a derivation of n enumerated steps is found by
layer n of the start side, and by layer ceil(n/2) when its second half uses rules with
converses (every rule but 2.22 and 2.28 has one). A meeting gives an ordinary ProofCertificate, checked as
prove's are. There is no depth cap and no time limit; when both sides close without meeting
the outcome is NOT_FOUND. COMPLETENESS.txt, section 6, states for which inputs it terminates.
In the shell: prove exhaustive [against] [TARGET].

3.3 Search over algebraic values

    prove_exhaustive(start, goal, ..., algebraic=True) -> ProofOutcome

runs archytaszx.rewrite.exhaustive.algebraic_search instead: rounds K = 1, 2, ..., each a search
of the kind of 3.2 from both sides to depth ceil(K/2), in which every free weight-vector variable of a
rule (the splits and insertions of the converses) also takes every vector whose entries are
algebraic numbers of level at most K (archytaszx.algebra.algebraic: rationals by height, roots
of unity by order, Gaussian rationals, then roots of irreducible integer polynomials by degree
plus largest coefficient). Where a rule's conditions determine the other free variables from the
first (zxw_s1_converse and x_fusion_converse: b = c / a coefficientwise when no coefficient of a
is 0; zxw_ad_converse: b = c - a), they are computed instead of enumerated. Diagrams are compared by algebraic_view (normal_form): every spider
parameter as weights and the scalar, each value in canonical form (a rational or CRootOf of its
minimal polynomial), so equal values compare equal. Both diagrams need concrete dimensions and
algebraic parameters (ProveGrammarError otherwise). Each round is finite; the rounds are not
bounded. COMPLETENESS.txt, section 6, states the termination theorem. In the shell: prove
exhaustive algebraic [against] [TARGET].

The kernel checks such a meet with the inference kernel.algebraic(a, b, cert): a node renaming
carries a onto b with every spider coefficient and the scalar equal in the number field of a
number-field certificate cert (ALGEBRA.txt, section 6), built from the values when not given and
checked either way. Each value is reduced to its pair (re, im) of polynomials in the field's
generator modulo p; values are compared by these pairs, never by sympy's comparison of CRootOf
forms. The theorem is a = a' (a with b's values; Inference "algebraic", data the certificate)
followed by iso(a', b). kernel.check of a proof certificate uses it at a meet that iso rejects,
and reports iso's error when both fail. The Lean export proves the step from p(θ) = 0
(LEAN.txt, section 4.1).


3.4 Mixed operands

prove on mixed operands first searches with the discard rules (tactics.mixed_moves) and returns
a ProofOutcome whose mixed field is a MixedCertificate of kind NATIVE; otherwise, and in
prove_exhaustive, it proves the doubled pair and returns kind DOUBLED (both doubled diagrams
with node origins and the inner pure proof). check_mixed(certificate) re-doubles a DOUBLED
certificate's sides id for id and checks the inner proof. The kernel refuses a pure rule's
step that consumes a discard; on mixed diagrams a Theorem states equality of CPMs and its iso
compares mixed scalars by modulus. MIXED.txt, section 5.


4. INDUCTION OVER MULTIPLICITIES: archytaszx.semantics.induction
----------------------------------------------------------------

    prove_by_induction(left, right, *, index=None, base=None, witness={},
                       ladder=DEFAULT_LADDER, rules=None, step_symbol="k", width=4,
                       max_steps=64, certify_proof=True, mode=EXACT, tolerance=1e-9,
                       max_elements=DEFAULT_MAX_ELEMENTS, restrict=None) -> InductionResult

proves an equality between two families for every value of one multiplicity symbol, index
(by default the lexicographically least free multiplicity). Every other symbol is either held
symbolic or fixed at the value witness gives it, for the tiers that need concrete values.
With restrict, the three steps of section 8: a clause n = v fixes a held symbol at v (the
index may not have one), a held value must lie inside its clause, and step 3 inducts the
restricted pair over the fresh symbol m of the index's clause from 0, so induction under
n >= c starts at n = c. InductionResult gains restriction, family, family_holds,
restricted_pair and counterexample_original.

4.1 The obligation

build_obligation constructs six diagrams: both sides at the base value (0 when both evaluate
there, otherwise 1), both sides at the step symbol k (the hypothesis), and both sides at k + 1
(the successor).

4.2 The step tiers

The base case is compared by the oracle. The step case is then discharged by the tiers of the
ladder, in order, until one settles:

  Tier                  Method                                                Verdict
  --------------------  ----------------------------------------------------  ----------------
  UNIFORM_REWRITE       rewrite the hypothesis diagrams into each other with  PROVED_UNIFORM
                        the multiplicity never instantiated
  INDUCTION_REWRITE     rewrite the successor diagrams into each other with   PROVED_INDUCTION
                        the hypothesis available as a rule; otherwise peel
                        one copy off each successor, discharge the residual
                        by the hypothesis and compare the peeled copies by
                        symbolic contraction
  SYMBOLIC_CONTRACTION  contract both successors with every symbol formal     PROVED_INDUCTION
  CHAIN_WINDOW          on a chain box (below)                                PROVED_INDUCTION
  TREE_WINDOW           on a tree box, over its depth (below)                 PROVED_INDUCTION
  ORACLE_WINDOW         compare by the oracle at every multiplicity in a      SCHEMA_CHECKED
                        window of width values

    Verdict: PROVED_UNIFORM | PROVED_INDUCTION | SCHEMA_CHECKED | REFUTED | INCONCLUSIVE

CHAIN_WINDOW peels the last copy off a successor whose step index is carried by one chain
box; a chain whose every link runs from an input p to an output q (copy m + 1 feeding copy m)
loses its first copy instead, laid out before the box's boundary block, the copy reading its
index as 0 and the residual as index + 1 (so an indexed residual is not the hypothesis). The
residual is joined to the copy by cut wires; find_cut_region places each cut end
among the residual's boundary slots so that the residual is isomorphic to that side's
hypothesis diagram. When both sides are chains read this way, with the residuals in the same
boundary places and the hypothesis diagrams laid out alike (boundary_layout), the step holds
when the two peeled copies, cut ends open and scalars over the hypothesis scalars, agree
(structurally or by symbolic contraction). Otherwise replace_cut_region swaps the residual for
the other side's hypothesis diagram, slot for slot, and the result is compared with the other
successor, peeled or not. replace_region and replace_cut_region refuse a replacement whose
boundary layout (per slot: the multiplicities replicating it and where each box's run starts)
differs from the hypothesis's.

When that fails and a successor's chain peels its first copy and reads an index, the window
is run again on offset_obligation: for a fresh natural s, both successors read their copy
index as index + s and both hypothesis diagrams as index + s + 1, so the peeled residual is
the hypothesis at s + 1. The claim inducted on is the pair at every s (P(n, s)); s = 0 is the
pair. The kernel rule induction(..., offset=s) checks s fresh and both sides at index 0
unchanged by s, rebuilds the successors and hypotheses so shifted, and records inference
kind induction_offset, which has no opaque Lean export. lean_chain.export_chain writes it
as chainNet families over every s: the copies read at k = 0 and offset 0 .. P - 1, main s k
giving level j of the chain at k the copy s + k - 1 - j (chain_equal on that copy function),
the instances linked at s = 0.

TREE_WINDOW peels the leaf level off a successor whose step index is carried by one tree box
(DIAGRAM.txt 4.2, fed box). Without the fed box, the tree's child ports back in its boundary
block (unfed), the rest must be isomorphic to that side's hypothesis diagram. fed_replacement
takes the other hypothesis diagram, finds its leaf run at the same slot counts before and after
the run, and grafts the fed box on: a single leg port-boxed b^k times loses its port box and is
wired to the fed box's feed port (one leg per copy), a tree box's child run of the same
branching and depth feeds it. The result is compared with the other successor, peeled or not,
structurally or by symbolic contraction.

PROVED_UNIFORM and PROVED_INDUCTION are proofs for every value of the index. SCHEMA_CHECKED is

4.4 The carry left open: archytaszx.semantics.carry_open

    open_carry(left, right, index) -> OpenCarry(left_open, right_open, cut, closed)
    carry_tail(diagram, index) -> (link-out ends, tail nodes)

The tail of left's chain box over index is every node off the box reached from its link-out
ends, apart from those reached from its link-in ends; it must lie in no box and hold no
boundary input. From each link-out end (sorted) a path U_i of F boxes and phase-free one-in
one-out Z or X spiders runs into the tail's finite part M, which meets nothing else and holds
every tail output slot. left_open (L') is left without the tail, its outputs left's outputs off
the tail, then the link-out ends; cut is L' as a region of left. right must hold, at M's output
slots, nodes S isomorphic to M through cut wires (find_cut_region), fed by wires only.
right_open (R') is right without S, the far end of S's input i continued by F^((4 - p_i) mod
4) (p_i the F count of U_i) and appended to its outputs. closed is left with L' replaced by R'
(replace_cut_region with contiguous=False). left = closed holds when L' = R' (kernel.congr),
and closed = right when M o U o U^-1 = M; the induction rung decides both, so a wrong R' only
leaves the pair unsettled.

    open_chain_ends(left, right, index, which) -> OpenEnds(left_open, right_open, context, cut,
                                                           closed)

On each side the wires from the link ends of the chain box over index to nodes off the box
are cut (which: both ends, the link-out ends, or the link-in ends, tried in that order), both
ends of each appended to the boundary list of their direction, link by link. context is left
with a phaseless Z(1 -> 1) on each cut wire; cut reads left_open as context without those
spiders; closed is context with that region replaced by right_open. The rung proves the open
pair by induction and decides left = context and closed = right without induction; the
theorem is trans(from_decision(left = context), congr(open-pair theorem, context, cut),
from_decision(closed = right)).

In the chain window, a peeled successor whose residual is not the hypothesis is tried again
with a fan spider split: a spider of the peeled copy wired to a boxed node off the copy and
holding a boundary slot becomes a hub (its phase, its legs off the copy but the slot, the
slot's place wired to a leaf) and a phaseless leaf (the slot and the legs into the copy); the
kernel induction exposes the split by the reversed spider fusion of hub and leaf.

A chain that peels its first copy is also peeled at its last copy (the carry-in end): that
side's residual read as its hypothesis through cut wires and replaced by the other side's,
the result compared with the other successor peeled at its last copy. When the chain cores
(box and head) are not separate, both diagrams are compared over the chain box scopes alone:
the scopes read as the hypothesis's box scope through cut wires, the rest of each, cut ends
open, by symbolic contraction (kernel.local_contraction). The kernel theorem
(induction_kernel._last_window) is the induction rule over the last-copy re-peel (repeel), its
step trans(local_contraction, symm(repeel of the other side)) when the lemma close fails.
The telescoping carry lemma U (open carry-in, end terms Zp(c_out, y) x y/d and Zp(c_in, y)
-x y/d^(n+1)) is proved this way for every n and d.

When no chain attempt settles, the chain window takes a plain box (no links) carrying the
step index the same way: that side's successor peeled, its residual read as its hypothesis
through cut wires (expose_cut) and replaced by the other side's, the result compared with the
other successor peeled, then unpeeled (iso, else symbolic contraction). The kernel theorem is
the chain_window one of section 6 (window cut).

Parametric induction. A phase parameter is a free symbol read only in node phases (real, not
a dimension, multiplicity, copy index or witness value). When the plain and offset chain
windows fail, induction.parameter_candidates solves, node by node, each hypothesis phase at
a = v against the same node's phase in the successor (linear in a, v free of copy and phase
indices, one value over all nodes of a side); for each value found the chain window runs
again with both hypothesis diagrams at a = v (induction.substitute_parameter: substituted,
expanded, powers combined). The claim inducted on is then "for every real a", so the
hypothesis at n = k holds at a = v. Example (tests/test_param_induction.py): a chain of n
Z(1 -> 1, d) phase{k: a 2^(j - n) k} equals Z phase{k: a (1 - 2^(-n)) k}; at n = k + 1 the
low k copies read a/2, so the hypothesis is taken at a = a/2. decide retries the step at an
empty witness when the sampled witness fixes a phase parameter (the base pair is decided with
it formal). Kernel: kernel.induction(..., parameter=(a, v)) re-checks a and v
(kernel._parameter_checked) and builds the hypothesis diagrams at a = v; inference kind
induction_param or induction_offset_param.

Constant generalisation (decide._generalized_induction). When the outermost induction rung
fails and the pair has no phase parameter, induction.generalized_constant takes the one
coefficient magnitude c (not 0 or 1) of the additive phase terms reading the index and puts a
fresh real a for c in every phase term +-c t; the lifted pair goes through the induction rung,
and its theorem is taken at a = c by kernel.phase_instance (inference kind phase_instance), then
joined to the original sides by iso. A lifted EQUAL without a kernel theorem at a = c is
dropped, so the later rungs decide the pair. Neither kind has a Lean export yet.

Cooley-Tukey lemma (lemmas.COOLEY_TUKEY, in lemmas.LIBRARY). For registers of dims s (top)
and t (low) bound by B(s, t): the shift by one over s t (X(1 -> 1, s t) phase{k: -k/(s t)})
between B(s, t) and S(s, t) equals the digit-wise form: on the low register a Z spider, the
shift by one over t, a Z spider; on the top register F, a Z spider of phase k/(s t), F^3;
pair phases x y/(s t) from that top spider to the low input spider and -x y/(s t) to the low
output spider. Proved once with s = d and t formal by symbolic contraction (lemmas.proved);
an instance at (s, t) is a symbolic contraction of the instantiated sides (at (2, 2^n),
(d, d^n), (d^n, d); at (d, d) the contraction is indeterminate and the instance fails).
lemmas.rewrite finds it at each binder B(s, t) feeding an X(1 -> 1) (Lemma.values), so the
induction lemma close (close_with_lemmas, rewrite_all) applies it. The form holds for an
integer shift; with the shift a real a the low part F, Z phase{k: a k/t}, F^3 and the left
side B, F, Z phase{k: a k/(s t)}, F^3, S are equal for every a, while X(1 -> 1) phase{k: -a
k/(s t)} differs from F Z F^3 at a non-integer a (at s = t = 2, a = 1/2).

Shift by two (lemmas.COOLEY_TUKEY_2, in lemmas.LIBRARY): the same form with the shift by two
over s t on the left, and on the right the top spider of phase 2 k/(s t), the low register
shifted by two over t, the pair phases unchanged. Its prover (lemmas._prove_two_shift) proves
it at any (s, t): the left side is two blocks B(s, t), shift by one, S(s, t) in series by
symbolic contraction, COOLEY_TUKEY is rewritten on each block, and the result is the right
side by symbolic contraction (the inner F^3 F cancels, the two inner pair phases cancel).
Lemma.proves_instances makes lemmas.instance call that prover at every value.

Radix chain lemmas (in lemmas.LIBRARY, d formal, for every p; lemmas.rewrite finds them at the
symbolic multiplicity of each chain box with a leg of dimension d^e, lemmas._radix_values):
  row_bind    a pair phase x y/d^(p + 1) between a d wire and the d^p register bound from p
              digits by a B(d^j, d) chain = a pair phase x y/d^(j + 2) on each digit j before
              the chain (the row in a box aligned with the chain).
  row_split   the same with -x y/d^(p + 1) on a register split by an S(d^j, d) chain, the row
              -x y/d^(j + 2) after the chain.
  top_bind    a top digit bound by B(1, d), then p digits by a B(d^(j + 1), d) chain = the p
              digits bound by a B(d^j, d) chain, then the top digit by B(d, d^p).
  top_split   the S form: S(d^(j + 1), d) chain and S(1, d) = S(d, d^p), then an S(d^j, d)
              chain.
row_bind and row_split are proved by decide_equal (induction, chain window); top_bind and
top_split by lemmas.induct_by_insertion, whose exposure reads the hypothesis directly through
cut wires when no Z spider off the copy meets it. The step contractions of top_bind and
top_split close by the ModDelta pin over a full index range (scalar._delta_step), whose range
test now reads coefficients that are integer expressions of known sign (scalar._bounds,
_signed_linear_over: i2 + d i3 with i2 < d, i3 < d^k lies in [0, d^(k + 1) - 1]).

The first copy of fca_boxed's box C0 (pair boxes P0, P3) and of the out -> in chain B(d^j, d)
peels by peel_one(first=True). kernel.repeel(source, index, a_last, b_last, a_first, b_first)
proves a first-copy peel equal to the last-copy peel of the same renamed source, both peels
recomputed by the kernel from source; a step proved at a first-copy peel enters
kernel.induction through trans with that repeel. Lean exports repeel as an axiom.

4.5 Periodic chains: decide._decide_periodic

When each side reads the index only in its scalar and in the multiplicity of at most one
top-level chain box, and each such chain's one-copy transfer matrix T (link-in values to
link-out values, exact, link dimensions concrete, no other open port, no copy index, copy
scalar included; contract_symbolic.chain_period) has T^p = c I for some p <= 8, both sides
scale by a constant from n to n + P (P the least common multiple of the periods; a scalar s
must satisfy s(n + P) = a s(n) for a constant a). When the two constants agree and the pairs
at n = 0 .. P - 1 are equal (decided without induction), the pair is EQUAL. The scaling test
lives in archytaszx.semantics.periodic (chain_period, shift_ratio, scaling). The theorem is
kernel.periodic(left, right, n, bases), bases[r] the theorem of the pair at n = r
(from_decision, else kernel.contraction); the kernel re-runs the scaling test with P =
len(bases) and checks each base's sides id for id against the sides at n = r. Inference kind
periodic; lean_periodic.export_periodic writes it to Lean (docs/LEAN.txt, PeriodicChain.lean).

In the tree window, a replaced diagram that does not close is tried again with W fusion
(induction.w_fused): a top-level box fed by one wire from the output q of a W node, its other
ports boundary outputs in one run, whose copy is exactly c W(1 -> m) after a Z(1 -> 1) with
weights w (ratios read on the copy's tensor, then checked by symbolic contraction), is
removed; q is port-boxed M m times in the run's place, the weighted Z goes before the W node
(then its only output), and the scalar gains (c s)^M, M the box's multiplicity and s its copy
scalar. W fusion and a weight on every leg equalling that weight on the input hold for W at
every dimension. The kernel rule w_fusion(d) proves d = w_fused(d), re-running w_fused (its
copy check by exact symbolic contraction included); the tree-window induction theorem takes
its step as w_fusion of the replaced diagram, then the usual step from the fused diagram to the
other peeled successor. Inference kind w_fusion; it has no Lean export yet.


5. CERTIFICATES: archytaszx.semantics.certificate
-------------------------------------------------

5.1 Structure

A Derivation is one node of a proof tree: an initial diagram, a final diagram and the evidence
between them. A STEP_SEQUENCE derivation carries RewriteSteps; an INDUCTION derivation carries
those over a free multiplicity, a base child, a step child and an InductionClaim (the symbol,
the base value, the symbols held symbolic, the tier that discharged the step). A Certificate
pairs a derivation with its CheckMethod: NUMERIC_ORACLE or INDUCTION.

    certify(initial, results, *, label="", check_method=NUMERIC_ORACLE) -> Certificate
    certify_induction(initial, final, results, base, step, claim, *, label="", ...)

5.2 Replay and verification

    replay(certificate, source=None, *, rediscover=True) -> ReplayResult
    verify(certificate, assignment, *, mode=EXACT, tolerance=1e-9, ...)
        -> VerificationReport

replay re-applies every recorded step to a fresh copy of the initial diagram and checks, per
step, that the rule still exists by name, that the consumed nodes and wires are present, that
the rule's pattern verifies the recorded match (Pattern.verify: by default the matcher
rediscovers it; a template rule re-checks it with its own free values), and that the step
apply produces equals the
recorded step; finally that the diagram reached is identical, id for id, to the recorded final
diagram. verify runs replay and then compares the two ends with the oracle at the assignment.

5.3 What a verified certificate establishes

  - The recorded steps re-derive the recorded final diagram exactly.
  - Every rule named still exists.
  - Every match is still found in the diagram it was recorded against.
  - Every recorded side-condition outcome and dimension constraint is what the matcher
    derives afresh.
  - For a NUMERIC_ORACLE certificate, the two ends denote the same tensor at the assignment.

It establishes nothing about other assignments, about the satisfiability of an assumed
dimension equation, or about a rule whose meaning changed under a name it kept. For an
INDUCTION certificate, the checked parts are the base case and one concrete instance of the
step; the step schema itself is discharged by the induction module and is not recorded in
re-checkable form. Tampering with any recorded field makes replay fail.


6. THE PROOF KERNEL: archytaszx.semantics.kernel

The kernel is a small set of inference rules. A Theorem states lhs = rhs for diagrams, every
free multiplicity universally quantified over the naturals, under listed assumptions. Only
these functions construct a Theorem:

                                     matcher rediscovers in d
  iso(a, b)                          a = b when their comparison views are isomorphic
  congr(t, context, cut)             context = context' when cut's region of context (its
                                     cut wires and its boundary slots and cut ends, each once)

                                     t.rhs; the opaque Lean export states the context as an
                                     axiom with two plug axioms and congrArg
  symm(t)                            rhs = lhs
  trans(a, b)                        a.lhs = b.rhs when a.rhs and b.lhs are identical
  induction(start, goal, index, step_symbol, base, side, expose, region, step, cut=None,
            tree=None)
                                     start = goal for every value of index, from a base
                                     theorem at 0, a theorem exposing one side's peeled
                                     successor at k + 1 as a context around that side at k,
                                     and a theorem from that context around the other side at
                                     k to the other peeled successor; the region is closed, or
                                     with cut read through its cut wires (replace_cut_region),
                                     or with tree (a tree box id) the exposed diagram without
                                     the box that tree feeds (unfed, fed_replacement)
  subst(t, clause, lhs=None, rhs=None)

                                     id with lhs and rhs when stated
  shift(t, left, right, clause)      left = right at n = m + c for every m, from t about both
                                     sides with the clause n >= c substituted (apply_clause),
                                     matched id for id or by isomorphic comparison views; its
                                     domain is the clause (used by the Lean export, LEAN.txt
                                     section 4.7)
  restricted(t, left, right, restriction, used=())

                                     must be the restricted pair or isomorphic to it; the floors
                                     in used become the assumptions "c <= d"

    refutation(decision, left, right) -> Refutation

Theorem.size() is the number of inferences (a subst counts one), Theorem.axioms() the rule
names of every rule inference in proof order (subst passes them through), and
Theorem.variables() the free multiplicities of both sides (m, not n, after n = a*m + c).
Theorem.domain holds the clauses that produced a restricted theorem, empty by default, and
Theorem.origin the unrestricted pair. Theorem.statement() is the printed claim: "for all n",
or per clause "for all n >= c (n = m + c)", "for all n = a*m + c (m >= 0)", "at n = v", then
"at d = v", "for d >= c" when the floor is an assumption "c <= d", and "for every d" when the
proof did not use the floor. An
induction certificate of the induction module carries no re-checkable step and is refused by
check; the shell then re-derives a re-checkable proof by search.

The induction rung of decide_equal builds Decision.induction_proof itself
(archytaszx.semantics.induction_kernel), its reason ending in "; kernel proof built" or
"; no kernel proof: <why>":

                        its rule that consumes the same nodes: a chain of rule, trans and iso
                        inferences, with no induction
  induction rewrite     an induction theorem: the base from the base decision's certificates
                        (from_decision), else a prove() search; the tier's peeled successor as
                        the exposed diagram (refl, or iso to the kernel's); its residual as the

                        (their normal forms meeting by iso, else a prove() search) re-applied
                        inside both successors

  tree_window           peeled successor exposed (refl), its residual through cut wires

                        replaced diagram to the other peeled successor by iso, else by their

An induction-rewrite step whose peeled copies have no derivation is the kernel's contraction of

The kernel inference contraction(a, b) re-runs contract_symbolic on both sides, every symbol
formal, and requires compare_symbolic to match (Inference "contraction"); local_contraction(a,
region_a, b, region_b, hypothesis) requires both regions to read as hypothesis through cut wires

their cut ends open. from_decision reads a SYMBOLIC_CONTRACTION decision as contraction(left,
right); Decision.proof tries the normaliser and the search before it. The opaque Lean export
proves a contraction over the networks of its contracted diagrams where the normaliser or a
path-sum network proof covers them (LEAN.txt, 4.5), else states it as an axiom.

For a family decided by symbolic contraction the normaliser proves the boxed pair by induction
on the box multiplicity (LEAN.txt, 4.3) when every node box meets the rest only at the boundary
or at the bundled inputs of Z spiders (with a boundary output or plain boundary input) and X
spiders (with a boundary output), a spider without such a leg allowed next to one of its colour
that has one. A point claim at n = v is then restricted_symbolic of that proof, exported as the
family theorem with its corollary at n = v, whose size and time do not depend on v:
bernstein_vazirani (and its restrictions n >= 1, d >= 2), phase_flip_code encoding,
repetition_decoding, phase_flip_decoding, parity_decoding, ghz_disentangle and deutsch_jozsa
constant (its scalar exp(-2 pi i/d) a constant root of unity, LEAN.txt 4.3); a point claim
of weyl_commutation at d = v likewise exports the theorem for every d with its corollary. A
family whose one indexed node box is read by one Z or X phase s j k / d per side (bv_secret_pattern)
is proved through its gadget pair, that phase pinned at a new boundary input, and stated over the
engine's diagrams as linked diagrams with the copy-indexed phase (LEAN.txt 4.2, lean_copyphase),
the point claim its corollary at n = v. The other
families of this kind (LEAN.txt, G6) keep the export of kernel.instantiated's pair, which grows
with v.

When no theorem is built the verdict's proof comes from the later builders of Decision.proof
(the normaliser, then search) as before.


7. TRUST MODEL
--------------

From least to most independent of the engine:

  1. A verdict of decide_equal or prove: trusts the engine's rewriting, matching and
     contraction code.
  2. A replayed certificate: trusts the rule definitions and the matcher, but re-derives every
     step.
  3. A kernel theorem: trusts the kernel's inference rules and the rule implementations it
     re-applies; a contraction inference also trusts contract_symbolic.
  4. A Lean proof over the tensor-network semantics: trusted are only Lean, Mathlib, the
     bundled library's definitions, and the translation of a diagram into a network. Every rule
     is a lemma proved in Lean; any step without one is an explicit axiom named unproved_*.

The oracle at every level is evidence at the points it evaluates, never a proof.


8. RESTRICTED DECISIONS: archytaszx.semantics.restrict
------------------------------------------------------

8.1 Clauses

A restriction is a set of clauses, at most one per symbol:

  Clause         Symbol kind     The set of values the claim covers
  -------------  --------------  ------------------------------------------------------
  n = v          multiplicity    {v}, v a non-negative integer literal
  n >= c         multiplicity    {c, c+1, ...}, c a non-negative integer literal
  n = a*m + c    multiplicity    {a*m + c : m >= 0}, a >= 1 and c >= 0 integer literals,
                                 m a fresh symbol (free in neither diagram, not used by
                                 another clause)
  d = v          dimension       {v}, v a positive integer literal
  d >= c         dimension       {c, c+1, ...}, c a positive integer literal

Symbols without a clause keep their domains (multiplicities >= 0, dimensions >= 1). n >= 0
and d >= 1 are accepted and equal no clause. A clause on a symbol free in neither diagram, a
second clause on one symbol, a non-fresh or reused m, a = 0, a negative literal, a dimension
clause other than d = v and d >= c, a dimension literal below 1, and a clause n = v or d = v
whose symbol a side binds to another value are DecideGrammarError naming the clause
(ERRORS.txt).

    parse_restriction(text | mapping | Restriction | None) -> Restriction
    validate(restriction, left, right) -> Restriction     kinds resolved, n >= c given its
                                                          fresh m
    in_domain(restriction, assignment) -> bool
    subst_pair(left, right, restriction) -> (Diagram, Diagram)
    apply_clause(diagram, clause) -> Diagram
    to_original(restriction, point), to_restricted(restriction, point)

8.2 Meaning

left =_R right means: for every assignment of the free symbols in their domains under R, the
two denotations are equal, scalar included. UNEQUAL under R means there is such an assignment
where they differ. A verdict printed for R never claims anything outside R; a stronger fact
found on the way (step 1 below) is printed as its own line.

8.3 The restricted pair

subst_R(D) is D with every clause substituted in order:

  1. n = v: every box carrying n instantiated at v (instantiate_symbol), n in the scalar and
     elsewhere set to v;
  2. n = a*m + c (n >= c is m + c): n replaced by a*m + c in every box multiplicity carrying
     it and in the scalar (n elsewhere is an error); every box whose multiplicity is concrete
     expanded, and every box whose multiplicity keeps a constant term c >= 1 peeled by peel_one
     until it has none, least box id first;
  3. d = v: Diagram.substitute({d: v}), so d is a literal and the concrete-only axioms match it;
  4. d >= c: nothing; the floor is recorded on the restriction.

The parameter environments lose the bindings of substituted symbols.

8.4 The three steps

decide_equal(left, right, restrict=R) runs:

  1. Family. The unrestricted decision on (left, right), floors ignored. EQUAL is EQUAL
     under R: family_holds is True, and the proof is the family proof specialised by subst.
  2. Counterexample in range. When step 1 is UNEQUAL with a counterexample inside R:
     UNEQUAL under R with that counterexample. An INTERFACE verdict is re-checked on the
     restricted pair and kept when it still holds there.
  3. Restricted pair. Otherwise the ladder on (subst_R(left), subst_R(right)), the sample
     grid, the symbolic witness search and the symbolic comparison cut to the floors of R.
     EQUAL is a theorem about the restricted pair; UNEQUAL carries a counterexample over m and,
     in counterexample_original, the same point in n = a*m + c.

prove exhaustive runs steps 1 and 3 side by side, in two threads stopped at their next layer
once the other decides: the family's PROVED or in-range REFUTED wins, otherwise the restricted
pair's outcome (an unprovable family claim never ends its search). samples_checked counts both steps.
With family_first=False (decide_equal, prove, prove_exhaustive, prove_by_induction; "only
for" in the REPL) steps 1 and 2 are skipped: step 3 runs alone, with the same samples and
floors; the result has restriction and restricted_pair set, family None and family_holds
False, and samples_checked counts step 3 only; prove exhaustive runs only the pair's search.
A point claim (every clause n = v or d = v) of pure diagrams with no interface mismatch and no
given samples first runs the oracle on the restricted pair at its sample grid, samples planned
above 2^20 elements skipped; on a mismatch steps 1 and 2 are skipped as with family_first=False.
The pair is not built when, read from the box multiplicities at the point without expanding
them, a side's boundary-dimension product is above 2^20 (every sample would be skipped) or a
side expands to more than 4096 nodes.
Certification takes the restricted-pair route. Clauses that are all identities make the flag
inert. Without clauses the decision is the unrestricted one,
field for field. Step 1 never uses a concrete-only axiom (its dimensions stay symbolic); they
match in step 3, where d = v is a literal.

8.5 Dimension floors

A clause d >= c raises the floor of d in the sample grid of step 3 (section 2.3) and is passed
to the symbolic rung: the witness search uses only in-floor assignments, and the contractions
are compared first without the floor, then under it. An EQUAL reached only under the floor
records it in Decision.floors_used and is exported with the hypothesis c <= d (LEAN.txt,
section 4.7). Rewriting rungs are unchanged. Floors and phase-vector floors combine by maximum.

8.6 Instances

A restriction binding every free multiplicity (n = v) yields a bang-box-free restricted pair.
With every dimension concrete too, the pair lies in the regime of COMPLETENESS.txt section 1,
and prove exhaustive algebraic under that restriction carries the termination theorem of
COMPLETENESS.txt section 6 for it.

8.7 Phase and exponent symbols, inequalities

Kinds: a multiplicity, a dimension, an exponent (free in a scalar and in no phase,
multiplicity or dimension) and a phase (free in a phase vector only), the most restrictive
role winning. Forms, for every kind: x = v, x != v, x >= c, x > c, x <= c, x < c, a <= x <= b
(strict variants too), x = a*m + c, x in Z, x in (1/q)Z, x in Z/q; integer literals for
multiplicities and dimensions, rationals for phases and exponents. The old forms on a
multiplicity or dimension keep sections 8.1-8.4 exactly, one clause per symbol; clauses of the
new forms, and every clause on a phase or exponent symbol, are intersected per symbol into an
interval (integer bounds for integer kinds, lattice bounds on a lattice), a lattice (1/q)Z and
excluded points, and planned:

  - one value: x = v substituted;
  - finitely many values (a bounded integer or lattice interval, or a phase symbol on (1/q)Z
    whose every phase expression changes by an integer when the symbol grows by 1): each value
    decided as its own instance; EQUAL when every instance is, UNEQUAL at the first unequal
    instance, else UNKNOWN naming the instances left; prove likewise: PROVED when every
    instance is proved, carrying each instance's outcome and certificate (instances), REFUTED
    at the first refuted instance, else NOT_FOUND naming the instances left;
  - a multiplicity or dimension with a lower bound: n >= c or the floor d >= c of 8.1, the
    excluded points a side condition;
  - a phase or exponent with a lower bound c: x = c + s (s >= 0, s > 0 when strict, s <= b - c
    a side condition when bounded above), with an upper bound only x = c - s, on a lattice the
    step s = k/q with k a fresh integer; x = a*m + c with m a fresh integer;
  - excluded points only: no substitution, a side condition.

An EQUAL of any rung holds for every value of the fresh symbol, hence on the restricted set,
and is kept and certified as in 8.4: the kernel theorem states each shift, side condition and
finite clause as a hypothesis ("for all alpha = s (s >= 0, s <= 1/2; alpha >= 0, alpha <=
1/2)", "for n >= 1, n in Z, n != 2"), and the Lean corollary main_restricted takes them as
hypotheses on its binders (0 ≤ s, s ≤ 1/2, n ≠ 2; a lattice step k : ℤ). A claim decided
instance by instance (a bounded domain or a periodic phase lattice) is EQUAL when every
instance is EQUAL; its proof is a kernel.FiniteProof, one RestrictedProof per value
combination of the finite clauses (certify_decision.finite_proof). kernel.finite_symbolic
rejects a FiniteProof whose instances are not exactly those combinations, in order, or whose
instance is restricted to other values or about another pair; it writes one Lean file
(lean_finite.combine): each instance's export with its top-level namespaces tagged I<k>, then
main_finite in the first instance's namespace. When every instance is the family proof's
corollary and one finite multiplicity or dimension has natural values over which the
instance statements agree, main_finite states that statement for every value (lo ≤ n → n ≤ hi
→ ..., or n = v1 ∨ n = v2 → ... for values with gaps) and proves it by rcases on the values
(omega from the bounds); otherwise each instance's statement is finite_claim in its namespace
and main_finite is their conjunction. An instance without a proof leaves the claim EQUAL
(uncertified), naming that instance ("instances without a proof: n = 2 (...)"); an instance
EQUAL (uncertified) by the ladder does the same.

The fresh symbol of a shift carries its lower bound as sympy assumptions: real and
nonnegative for s >= 0, positive for s > 0, integer (and nonnegative) on a lattice. Every rung
reads them, so a comparison that holds only on the restricted range (sqrt(e^2) = e for e >= 0,
or for 0 <= e <= 2) decides there; the bound is the hypothesis the theorem states. When these
assumptions make a substituted scalar leave the scalar grammar (an Abs), the shift is
substituted with a plain symbol instead. An upper bound s <= b - c and excluded points have
no sympy encoding; a restricted pair the ladder leaves UNKNOWN under them is EQUAL_UNCERTIFIED
when its symbolic contractions' entry difference, a polynomial in its index atoms and the
index symbols outside them, has every
coefficient vanish on the real range of the single symbol left (a <= s <= b without the
excluded points): reduce_inequalities finds no point of the range where a real part is above
or below 0, and solveset finds every point of it a root (|e - 1| = 1 - e for -1 <= e <= 1).
A range point a lattice symbol does not take is checked too, which only strengthens the
check. A multiplicity floor n >= c (or n = a*m + c) with excluded points gives its fresh m
the range m >= 0 without the excluded points read in m ((n^2 - 9)/(n - 3) = n + 3 for
n >= 1, n != 3). With two or more symbols left, each with a range (a half-line shift
included) and one of them bounded or punctured, every |g| of one symbol is replaced by g or
-g when reduce_inequalities shows g of one sign on that symbol's range, and the rest must
simplify to 0 (|e - 1| |f - 1| = (1 - e)(1 - f) for -1 <= e <= 1, -1 <= f <= 1); when it
does not, the points built from each range's midpoint and quarter points are tried as
oracle candidates (UNEQUAL at e = 0, f = 2 for -1 <= f <= 3). The oracle candidate takes the
default samples' values for the other symbols (d). The kernel has no proof of the vanishing. When the check fails because a real part is
above or below 0 somewhere in the range, a rational point there (an interval's midpoint, one
past a finite end of a half-line) goes to the oracle; a mismatch there is an UNEQUAL, its
point read in the original symbols and kept only inside the restriction (|e - 2| = 2 - e for
0 <= e <= 3: UNEQUAL at e = 5/2). An UNEQUAL is kept
only when its point, read in the original symbols, satisfies every clause and side condition
(in_domain), else UNKNOWN; the restricted pair's oracle samples only points inside. prove and
induct use the same plans; a refutation outside the restriction is not found, and a claim
with finitely many instances is decided, not proved, when the family proof fails.
